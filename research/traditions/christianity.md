# Christianity (Gospels, Paul, James, early Fathers)

## Overview

Christianity began as a movement within Second Temple Judaism around Jesus of Nazareth (c. 4 BCE-30 CE), and its ethics grow directly out of the Hebrew Bible and contemporary Jewish teaching. The Gospels (Mark c. 70 CE; Matthew and Luke c. 80-90; John c. 90-100) present Jesus's teaching on the **basileia tou theou** ("kingdom/reign of God"). That teaching is summed up in the double love command (Deut 6:5 + Lev 19:18) and radicalized in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7) and Sermon on the Plain (Luke 6): love of enemies, non-retaliation, secret almsgiving, purity of heart. Central Greek terms: **agapē** (self-giving love), **alētheia** (truth; in John, something one _does_), **phōs / skotos** (light/darkness), **dikaiosynē** (righteousness/justice), **metanoia** (change of mind, repentance), **charis** (grace), **eleēmosynē** (almsgiving, mercy).

Paul's letters (c. 50-60 CE, the earliest Christian writings) ground ethics in being "in Christ". Love is "the fulfilling of the law" (Rom 13:10), and believers are "children of light" (Eph 5:8, 1 Thess 5:5). The Letter of James insists that faith without works is dead. The Johannine writings (Gospel, 1-3 John) build a vivid light/darkness dualism, close in idiom to the Dead Sea Scrolls, in which love of one's brother is the test of being "in the light". That makes them natural companions to the already-chosen John 3:19-21.

The early Fathers carried this into practical instruction. The _Didache_ (late 1st/early 2nd century) opens with the Jewish "Two Ways" teaching. Augustine of Hippo (354-430) made love (_caritas_) the root and criterion of all action ("Love, and do what you will") and wrote searchingly about why people love truth when it illumines them but hate it when it exposes them.

## Candidate passages

Normalization: NT text is copied from the WEB verse-per-line file (https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-web_vpl.zip) and verified programmatically against each chapter page cited. Verse numbers, tags and footnote markers are stripped, and the WEB's typographic (curly) quotes and apostrophes are typed here as straight ones. Restore the curly forms from the source if exact typography matters. Patristic texts have CCEL/Gutenberg footnote numbers and Scripture-reference notes stripped and whitespace collapsed; otherwise verbatim.

John 3:19-21 is already chosen and deliberately excluded. The selection leans toward passages that answer it: light, truth, deeds done openly or in secret.

### 1. Matthew 5:14-16

> "You are the light of the world. A city located on a hill can't be hidden. Neither do you light a lamp and put it under a measuring basket, but on a stand; and it shines to all who are in the house. Even so, let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/MAT05.htm
- **Why:** The positive face of John 3:21: good works belong in the light. The opening quote mark is the start of Jesus's speech; drop it for display.

### 2. Matthew 6:3-4

> But when you do merciful deeds, don't let your left hand know what your right hand does, so that your merciful deeds may be in secret, then your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/MAT06.htm
- **Why:** The deliberate counterweight to Matt 5:16: do good openly but not _for display_. It makes a fine paired tension with John 3:21, and it parallels Qur'an 2:271 closely.

### 3. Luke 8:16-17

> "No one, when he has lit a lamp, covers it with a container or puts it under a bed; but puts it on a stand, that those who enter in may see the light. For nothing is hidden that will not be revealed, nor anything secret that will not be known and come to light.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/LUK08.htm
- **Why:** "Nothing is hidden that will not be revealed" is the same exposure-by-light logic as John 3:20.

### 4. Ephesians 5:8-9

> For you were once darkness, but are now light in the Lord. Walk as children of light, for the fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and truth,

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/EPH05.htm
- **Why:** Light defined ethically as "goodness and righteousness and truth". Note that 5:9 ends with a comma (5:10 continues). Verified on the same page: 5:13, "But all things, when they are reproved, are revealed by the light, for everything that reveals is light."

### 5. 1 John 1:5-7

> This is the message which we have heard from him and announce to you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. If we say that we have fellowship with him and walk in the darkness, we lie and don't tell the truth. But if we walk in the light as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanses us from all sin.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/1JN01.htm
- **Why:** The same Johannine community as John 3. "Walking in the light" means honesty and fellowship. Verses 5-6 stand well alone if a less creedal passage is wanted.

### 6. 1 John 3:18

> My little children, let's not love in word only, or with the tongue only, but in deed and truth.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/1JN03.htm
- **Why:** One line joining love, deed and truth ("doing the truth", as in John 3:21). Also verified: 1 John 2:10, "He who loves his brother remains in the light, and there is no occasion for stumbling in him." (https://ebible.org/eng-web/1JN02.htm)

### 7. James 2:15-17

> And if a brother or sister is naked and in lack of daily food, and one of you tells them, "Go in peace. Be warmed and filled;" yet you didn't give them the things the body needs, what good is it? Even so faith, if it has no works, is dead in itself.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/JAS02.htm
- **Why:** Concrete, almost comic, and devastating. Also verified: James 1:22, "But be doers of the word, and not only hearers, deluding your own selves." (https://ebible.org/eng-web/JAS01.htm)

### 8. Luke 6:35-36

> But love your enemies, and do good, and lend, expecting nothing back; and your reward will be great, and you will be children of the Most High; for he is kind toward the unthankful and evil. "Therefore be merciful, even as your Father is also merciful.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/LUK06.htm
- **Why:** Enemy-love grounded in imitating God's mercy, very close to the rabbinic imitatio Dei (Sotah 14a). Also verified: Luke 6:31, "As you would like people to do to you, do exactly so to them." Matthew 7:12 (https://ebible.org/eng-web/MAT07.htm): "Therefore, whatever you desire for men to do to you, you shall also do to them; for this is the law and the prophets."

### 9. Romans 12:21

> Don't be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/ROM12.htm
- **Why:** The shortest, most portable statement of non-retaliation. The context (12:20) quotes Proverbs 25:21-22. Also verified: 12:17, "Repay no one evil for evil. Respect what is honorable in the sight of all men."

### 10. Didache 1:1-2 (The Two Ways)

> There are two ways, one of life and one of death; but a great difference between the two ways. 2. The way of life, then, is this: First, thou shalt love God who made thee; second, thy neighbour as thyself; and all things whatsoever thou wouldst should not occur to thee, thou also to another do not do.

- **Translation:** M. B. Riddle, _Ante-Nicene Fathers_ vol. 7 (1886), PD
- **Source URL:** https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/anf07/anf07.viii.iii.i.html
- **Why:** The oldest Christian moral catechism keeps the **negative** golden rule (like Hillel) and the Deuteronomic two ways. It is the bridge text between Jewish and Christian ethics.

### 11. Augustine, _Homilies on 1 John_, Homily 7 §8

> Once for all, then, a short precept is given thee: Love, and do what thou wilt: whether thou hold thy peace, through love hold thy peace; whether thou cry out, through love cry out; whether thou correct, through love correct; whether thou spare, through love do thou spare: let the root of love be within, of this root can nothing spring but what is good.

- **Translation:** H. Browne, _NPNF_ 1st series vol. 7 (1888), PD
- **Source URL:** https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf107/npnf107.iv.x.html
- **Why:** Ethics of the inner root rather than outward appearance. The preceding sentences ("thorns also have flowers...") make clear this is about acting from love, including hard correction, not license.

### 12. Augustine, _Confessions_ X.23 (§34)

> They love truth when she enlightens, they hate her when she reproves. For since they would not be deceived, and would deceive, they love her when she discovers herself unto them, and hate her when she discovers them.

- **Translation:** E. B. Pusey (1838), PD
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3296/pg3296.txt
- **Why:** A psychological commentary on John 3:19-20 itself (_amant eam lucentem, oderunt eam redarguentem_). It may be the best single companion to the already-chosen passage. The section number follows the standard Latin division; the Gutenberg file carries no section numbers, so confirm X.23.34 against a numbered edition before display.

## Beware

- **"Preach the Gospel at all times; when necessary, use words"** is attributed to Francis of Assisi but not found in his writings or early biographies.
- **"Love the sinner, hate the sin"** is not biblical. It loosely echoes Augustine, Letter 211 (_cum dilectione hominum et odio vitiorum_, "with love for persons and hatred of vices"), and was popularized by Gandhi.
- **"Love, and do what you will"** is real (Homily 7 on 1 John) but routinely misused as a license slogan. Its context is loving correction.
- **"The glory of God is man fully alive"** is a paraphrase of Irenaeus, _Against Heresies_ IV.20.7 (_gloria Dei vivens homo_, "the glory of God is a living man"), which continues "and the life of man is the vision of God".
- **"God helps those who help themselves"** is not biblical (Franklin/Sidney).
- **"Money is the root of all evil"** misquotes 1 Tim 6:10 ("the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil").
- Many popular "Augustine" lines (e.g. "The world is a book and those who do not travel read only one page") have no source in his works.

## Bridges

1. **Light that exposes.** Augustine (_Confessions_ X.23) and Luke 8:17 extend John 3:19-21. Compare **Hebrew Bible**, Proverbs 20:27 ("The spirit of man is Yahweh's lamp, searching all his innermost parts"), and **Islam**, the hadith "sin is what pricks within thy bosom, and what thou wouldst be averse to make public" (Nawawi 27): the same test of whether a deed can bear the light.
2. **Open vs secret goodness (a productive tension).** Matthew 5:16 ("let your light shine") vs Matthew 6:3-4 ("in secret") is mirrored almost exactly in **Islam**, Qur'an 2:271 ("If ye publish your almsgiving, it is well, but if ye hide it ... it will be better for you"), and in **Rabbinic** Avot 1:3 (serve "without a view to receive recompense").
3. **Golden rule, negative and positive.** Luke 6:31 / Matthew 7:12 (positive) vs **Didache 1:2** and **Rabbinic**, Hillel at Shabbat 31a (negative). Also **Confucianism**, _Analects_ 15:24, and **Islam**, Nawawi 13.
4. **Overcoming evil with good.** Romans 12:21 (quoting **Hebrew Bible**, Proverbs 25:21-22) pairs with **Islam**, Qur'an 41:34 ("Repel the evil deed with one which is better"), and **Daoism**, _Tao Te Ching_ 63 (on answering injury with virtue/de; not fetched, so verify the wording before quoting).
5. **Faith shown in deeds.** James 2:15-17 and 1 John 3:18 pair with **Rabbinic**, Avot 1:17 ("not learning but doing is the groundwork"), and **Islam**, Qur'an 107 (those "who would be seen (at worship) / Yet refuse small kindnesses").
6. **Two ways and death.** Didache 1:1 echoes **Hebrew Bible**, Deuteronomy 30:19, and has an analogue in **Zoroastrianism**'s choice between asha and druj (Yasna 30). On mortality, Augustine's "our heart is restless, until it repose in Thee" (_Confessions_ I.1, verified in the same Gutenberg file) contrasts with **Epicurean** calm in the face of death.
