# Hindu Dharma: Upanishads, Gita, Mahabharata, Manusmriti

## Overview

"Hinduism" is a modern umbrella term for a family of South Asian traditions that
call their own way _sanātana dharma_ (सनातन धर्म, "the eternal order/duty"). The
ethical vocabulary that runs through the texts in this dossier is:
_dharma_ (धर्म, right order, duty, righteousness), _satya_ (सत्य, truth, "the
real"), _ahiṃsā_ (अहिंसा, non-injury), _dayā_ / _karuṇā_ (दया / करुणा,
compassion), _dama_ (दम, self-restraint), _dāna_ (दान, giving), and _karma_
(कर्म, action and its consequence). The metaphysical core of the Upanishads
(the Vedānta, "end of the Veda") is that the inner self, _ātman_ (आत्मन्), is
not finally separate from _brahman_ (ब्रह्मन्), the ground of all being. That
identity becomes an ethical claim: whoever sees the one self in all beings
cannot hate any of them (Īśā 6-7, Gītā 6.32).

Light and darkness are central images. The Upanishadic prayer _tamaso mā
jyotir gamaya_ ("from darkness lead me to light") treats ignorance (_avidyā_,
अविद्या) as darkness (_tamas_, तमस्) and knowledge as light (_jyotis_, ज्योतिस्).
In the Gita the divine dwells in "darkened souls" and dispels ignorance with
"the lamp of knowledge" (_jñāna-dīpa_, ज्ञानदीप). The Mahabharata's
Mokshadharma says outright that "untruth is only another form of darkness."
These are the closest resonances in this tradition to John 3:19-21.

Historical setting: the oldest Upanishads (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya) are
usually dated to roughly the 7th-6th centuries BCE, with verse Upanishads (Kaṭha,
Īśā, Muṇḍaka, Śvetāśvatara) somewhat later. The Mahabharata grew over several
centuries (c. 400 BCE to 400 CE); the Bhagavad Gītā is its sixth-book dialogue
between Krishna and the warrior Arjuna. The _Mānava Dharmaśāstra_ (Manusmṛti)
is a Brahmanical legal-ethical treatise, c. 2nd century BCE to 2nd century CE.
**Handle Manu with care:** it contains admirable maxims about truthfulness and
self-restraint alongside rules about caste and women that many Hindus today
reject and that were burned in protest by B. R. Ambedkar in 1927. Any Manu
passage displayed should stand on its own and not imply endorsement of the
whole code.

## Candidate passages

Notes on method: every passage below was fetched on 2026-11-10 and copied from
the saved file. For Hume (archive.org OCR) I collapsed OCR double-spacing and

### 1. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upanishad 1.3.28 (the _pavamāna_ prayer)

> "From the unreal (asat) lead me to the real (sat)!
> From darkness lead me to light!
> From death lead me to immortality!"

- Translation: R. E. Hume, _The Thirteen Principal Upanishads_ (1921), Bṛhad. 1.3.28. PD.
- Source fetched: https://archive.org/download/thirteenprincipa00hume/thirteenprincipa00hume_djvu.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/बृहदारण्यक_उपनिषद्_1a): असतो मा सद्गमय, तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय, मृत्योर्मामृतं गमयेति ।
- Why: The single most direct parallel to John 3:19-21 in Indian scripture: truth, light and life paired against unreality, darkness and death. Still recited daily by millions.

### 2. Bhagavad Gītā 5.15-16 (Arnold)

> Mankind errs here
> By folly, darkening knowledge. But, for whom
> That darkness of the soul is chased by light,
> Splendid and clear shines manifest the Truth
> As if a Sun of Wisdom sprang to shed
> Its beams of dawn.

- Translation: Edwin Arnold, _The Song Celestial_ (1885), Chapter V. PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2388/pg2388.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/भगवद्गीता/कर्मसंन्यासयोगः): अज्ञानेनावृतं ज्ञानं तेन मुह्यन्ति जन्तवः ॥५-१५॥ … ज्ञानेन तु तदज्ञानं येषां नाशितमात्मनः । तेषामादित्यवज्ज्ञानं प्रकाशयति तत्परम् ॥५-१६॥
- Why: Ignorance as a darkness over knowledge, truth rising like the sun once it is lifted. Strong light/truth pairing.

### 3. Bhagavad Gītā 10.10-11 (Arnold)

> And unto these--thus serving well, thus loving ceaselessly--
> I give a mind of perfect mood, whereby they draw to Me;
> And, all for love of them, within their darkened souls I dwell,
> And, with bright rays of wisdom's lamp, their ignorance dispel.

- Translation: Arnold (1885), Chapter X. PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2388/pg2388.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/भगवद्गीता/विभूतियोगः): तेषामेवानुकम्पार्थमहमज्ञानजं तमः । नाशयाम्यात्मभावस्थो ज्ञानदीपेन भास्वता ॥१०-११॥
- Why: Light given out of compassion (_anukampā_), dwelling inside the dark rather than condemning it. A gentle counterpart to John's "light is come into the world."

### 4. Kaṭha Upanishad 2.2.15 (Paramananda: Part Fifth, XV)

> The sun does not shine there, nor the moon, nor the stars; nor do
> these lightnings shine there, much less this fire. When He
> shines, everything shines after Him; by His light all is lighted.

- Translation: Swami Paramananda, _The Upanishads_ (1919). PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3283/pg3283.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/कठोपनिषत्/द्वितीयोध्यायः/द्वितीयवल्ली): न तत्र सूर्यो भाति न चन्द्रतारकं नेमा विद्युतो भान्ति कुतोऽयमग्निः । तमेव भान्तमनुभाति सर्वं तस्य भासा सर्वमिदं विभाति ॥ १५ ॥
- Why: The same verse recurs in Muṇḍaka 2.2.10 and Śvetāśvatara 6.14. Compare John 1:9 and Revelation 21:23 ("the city had no need of the sun").

### 5. Īśā Upanishad 15 (Paramananda)

> The face of Truth is hidden by a golden disk. O Pushan
> (Effulgent Being)! Uncover (Thy face) that I, the worshipper of
> Truth, may behold Thee.

- Translation: Paramananda (1919). PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3283/pg3283.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/ईशादिविंशोत्तरशतोपनिषदः/उपनिषद‌-०१-१०): हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण सत्यस्यापिहितं मुखम् ॥ तत्त्वं पूषन्नपावृणु सत्यधर्माय दृष्टये ॥१५॥
- Why: "The worshipper of Truth" (_satya-dharma_) asks to see truth face to face, even though the dazzle covers it. Close to "he that doeth truth cometh to the light."

### 6. Muṇḍaka Upanishad 3.1.6 (Hume)

> Truth alone conquers, not falsehood.
> By truth is laid out the path leading to the gods (devayana)
> By which the sages whose desire is satisfied ascend
> To where is the highest repository of truth.

- Translation: Hume (1921). PD.
- Source: https://archive.org/download/thirteenprincipa00hume/thirteenprincipa00hume_djvu.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/मुण्डकोपनिषद्): सत्यमेव जयते नानृतं सत्येन पन्था विततो देवयानः । येनाऽऽक्रमन्त्यृषयो ह्याप्तकामा यत्र तत् सत्यस्य परमं निधानम् ॥ ३.१.६ ॥
- Why: _Satyameva jayate_ is India's national motto. Truth as a road that is walked, not only believed.

### 7. Taittirīya Upanishad 1.11.1-2 (Hume)

> Speak the truth.
> Practise virtue (dharma).
> …
> Be one to whom a mother is as a god.
> Be one to whom a father is as a god.
> Be one to whom a teacher is as a god.
> Be one to whom a guest is as a god.

- Translation: Hume (1921). PD. (Ellipsis omits lines on study and progeny; the guest lines are on the following page of the scan.)
- Source: https://archive.org/download/thirteenprincipa00hume/thirteenprincipa00hume_djvu.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/तैत्तिरीयोपनिषदत्/शिक्षावल्ली): सत्यं वद । धर्मं चर । … मातृदेवो भव । पितृदेवो भव । आचार्यदेवो भव । अतिथिदेवो भव ।
- Why: The teacher's parting charge to a graduating student. _Atithi devo bhava_ ("the guest as god") is a living proverb.

### 8. Īśā Upanishad 6-7 (Paramananda)

> He who sees all beings in the Self and the Self in all beings, he
> never turns away from It (the Self).
>
> He who perceives all beings as the Self for him how can there be
> delusion or grief, when he sees this oneness (everywhere)?

- Translation: Paramananda (1919). PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3283/pg3283.txt
- Sanskrit (sa.wikisource Īśā page above; transcription has typos, e.g. सर्वानि for सर्वाणि): यस्तु सर्वानि भूतान्यात्मन्येबानुपश्यति ॥ सर्वभूतेषु चात्मानं ततो न विजुगुप्सते ॥६॥ **Check against a printed edition before display.**
- Why: The metaphysical root of Hindu non-hatred; _na vijugupsate_ literally "does not shrink from / loathe" anyone.

### 9. Mahābhārata, Anuśāsana Parva, section CXIII (Ganguli numbering): the golden rule

> One should never do that to another which one regards as
> injurious to one's own self. This, in brief, is the rule of
> Righteousness. One by acting in a different way by yielding to desire,
> becomes guilty of unrighteousness.

- Speaker: Bṛhaspati, preceptor of the gods, to Yudhiṣṭhira.
- Translation: K. M. Ganguli (1883-96). PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15477/pg15477.txt (Vol. 4)
- Why: The tradition's own crisp statement of the golden rule. In the Pune Critical Edition this is usually cited as 13.114.8.

### 10. Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva (Mokṣadharma), section CXC (Ganguli numbering)

> Truth is Brahma; Truth is Penance; it is Truth that
> creates all creatures. It is by Truth that the whole universe is upheld;
> and it is with the aid of Truth that one goes to heaven. Untruth is only
> another form of Darkness. It is Darkness that leads downwards. Those who
> are afflicted by Darkness and covered by it fail to behold the lighted
> regions of heaven. It has been said that Heaven is Light and that Hell is
> Darkness.

- Speaker: the sage Bhṛgu.
- Translation: Ganguli. PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15476/pg15476.txt (Vol. 3)
- Why: Nearly a gloss on John 3:19-21: untruth _is_ darkness; those covered by it cannot see the light. Could be trimmed to the last three sentences.

### 11. Bhagavad Gītā 12.13-14 (Arnold)

> Who hateth nought
> Of all which lives, living himself benign,
> Compassionate, from arrogance exempt,
> Exempt from love of self, unchangeable
> By good or ill; patient, contented, firm
> In faith, mastering himself, true to his word,
> Seeking Me, heart and soul; vowed unto Me,--
> That man I love!

- Translation: Arnold (1885), Chapter XII. PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2388/pg2388.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/भगवद्गीता/भक्तियोगः): अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च । निर्ममो निरहंकारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी ॥१२-१३॥
- Why: Portrait of the beloved devotee: no hatred, friendliness (_maitra_), compassion (_karuṇa_), humility (_nirahaṃkāra_). Strong humility and non-violence candidate.

### 12. Kaṭha Upanishad 1.2.1-2 (Paramananda: Part Second, I-II)

> Yama said: The good is one thing and the pleasant another. These
> two, having different ends, bind a man. It is well with him who
> chooses the good. He who chooses the pleasant misses the true
> end.
>
> The good and the pleasant approach man; the wise examines both
> and discriminates between them; the wise prefers the good to the
> pleasant, but the foolish man chooses the pleasant through love
> of bodily pleasure.

- Translation: Paramananda (1919). PD.
- Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3283/pg3283.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/कठोपनिषत्/प्रथमोध्यायः/द्वितीयवल्ली): अन्यच्छ्रेयोऽन्यदुतैव प्रेयस्ते उभे नानार्थे पुरुषँ सिनीतः । तयोः श्रेय आददानस्य साधु भवति हीयतेऽर्थाद्य उ प्रेयो वृणीते ॥ १ ॥
- Why: Death himself (Yama) teaching the boy Naciketas. The _śreyas / preyas_ (good vs. pleasant) choice is a classic statement of moral discernment.

## Bridges

1. **Light and darkness / John 3:19-21.** BAU 1.3.28 (_tamaso mā jyotir gamaya_) and Mahābhārata Śānti CXC ("Untruth is only another form of Darkness … Heaven is Light and … Hell is Darkness") line up closely with John's claim that people love darkness because their deeds are evil. The difference: in Vedānta the root of darkness is mainly ignorance (_avidyā_) rather than a will that hates the light, and Gītā 10.11 has the divine entering the darkness to light it from within.
2. **Sincerity and truth.** _Satyameva jayate_ (Muṇḍaka 3.1.6) and "Speak the truth" (Taittirīya 1.11) beside Confucian _cheng_ 誠 (Doctrine of the Mean 20), Shinto _makoto_, and Jesus's "the truth shall make you free" (John 8:32). Tension: Manu 4.138 advises against "disagreeable truth", while Hebrew prophets and Gandhi's _satyāgraha_ insist on it.
3. **The golden rule.** Mahābhārata Anuśāsana CXIII sits beside Confucius (_Analects_ 15.24, _shu_ 恕), Hillel (Shabbat 31a), Matthew 7:12, and the Jain Ācārāṅga 1.5.5 ("As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to kill"). Gītā 6.32's _ātmaupamya_ ("taking oneself as the measure") is the same principle in Yoga terms.
4. **Non-violence and its tension.** The Mahābhārata repeatedly calls _ahiṃsā_ the highest dharma (Anuśāsana CXV, Ganguli: "Abstention from injury is the highest religion"), yet the Gītā is spoken on a battlefield to persuade a warrior to fight. Jains and Buddhists made _ahiṃsā_ absolute. Gandhi read the battle as allegory, which makes a good discussion point against the Sermon on the Mount.
5. **Death.** Gītā 2.20 ("Death hath not touched it at all") and the Kaṭha Upanishad's frame (a boy questioning Death himself) compare with 1 Corinthians 15:55, Socrates in the _Phaedo_, and the Buddhist teaching of _anattā_, which denies the very permanent self that the Gītā affirms. That is a real and interesting tension.
6. **Humility.** Gītā 12.13 (_nirahaṃkāra_, "from arrogance exempt") with the Beatitudes ("Blessed are the meek") and Daodejing 8 (water benefits all and does not contend).

## Further reading

- Patrick Olivelle, _Upaniṣads_ (Oxford World's Classics, 1996), and _Manu's Code of Law: A Critical Edition and Translation_ (OUP, 2005). Current scholarly standard for both texts.
- Gavin Flood, _An Introduction to Hinduism_ (Cambridge UP, 1996). Balanced overview, good on dharma.
- Franklin Edgerton, _The Bhagavad Gītā_ (Harvard Oriental Series, 1944). Literal translation plus interpretation.
- Alf Hiltebeitel, _Dharma: Its Early History in Law, Religion, and Narrative_ (OUP, 2011). How _dharma_ shifts between the Veda, epics and Manu.
- Wendy Doniger, _The Hindus: An Alternative History_ (Penguin, 2009). Useful for dissenting and non-Brahmanical voices (read alongside more traditional accounts; it is controversial among some Hindu groups).

## Beware (misattributions and mis-renderings)

- **"Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds."** J. Robert Oppenheimer's own paraphrase of Gītā 11.32, not a standard translation. _Kāla_ there is "Time".
- **"Ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ, dharma hiṃsā tathaiva ca"** ("…and so too is violence in service of dharma"). The second half circulates widely online but is not found in the Mahābhārata; the first half is genuine.
- **Internet "Gita quotes"** such as "Change is the law of the universe" or "Whatever happened, happened for the good; whatever is happening, is happening for the good…" are modern inspirational posters, not Gītā verses.
- **"Vasudhaiva kuṭumbakam"** ("the world is one family") is real but comes from the Mahā Upaniṣad and the _Hitopadeśa_, not the Rig Veda as often claimed.
- **Arnold's numbering:** Arnold does not mark verses and sometimes adds lines (e.g. "[Who will may have this Light; Who hath it sees.]" near 5.17-18). Always cite chapter.verse from the Sanskrit and say the English is Arnold's rendering.
- **Ganguli section numbers** follow the vulgate; the Pune Critical Edition numbers differ, so cite "Anuśāsana Parva, sec. CXIII (Ganguli)".
