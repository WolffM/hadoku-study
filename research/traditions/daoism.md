# Daoism (道家 / 道教)

## Overview

Daoism takes its name from 道 _dao_, "the Way": the nameless source and pattern of all things, which can't be grasped by argument and can be followed only by attunement. Its ethics comes from imitating that Way. The sage practises 無為 _wuwei_ ("non-action", or action without contrivance or striving), 自然 _ziran_ ("so-of-itself", spontaneity), and keeps to the low place like 水 water. He cultivates 德 _de_ (virtue, or inner potency), 樸 _pu_ (the uncarved block, unadorned simplicity), 柔 _rou_ (softness) and 靜 _jing_ (stillness). The Daodejing (道德經) names "three treasures" (三寶 _sanbao_): 慈 _ci_ (compassion or gentleness, "deep love"), 儉 _jian_ (frugality) and 不敢為天下先 (not presuming to be first under heaven). Against Confucian moral rhetoric it warns that loudly proclaimed 仁義 _ren-yi_ (humaneness and righteousness) are signs that the Way has already been lost. The ideal is a person whose goodness doesn't call attention to itself: 光而不燿, "bright, but not dazzling."

Light is a quiet but recurring image, and it fits the owner's first choice, John 3:19-21. 明 _ming_ (clarity, illumination) is what you get by knowing oneself (自知者明, ch. 33) and "knowing the constant" (知常曰明, ch. 16). Chapter 52 counsels "using one's light and returning to its brightness" (用其光，復歸其明). The Zhuangzi speaks of a settled heart that "emits a heavenly light" (發乎天光), and it says hidden wrongdoing is not really hidden: what men don't punish, the spirits do (ch. 23). The Zhuangzi (莊子) also contributes radical equanimity before death, an emphasis on 真 _zhen_ (authenticity, "the genuine": "the perfection of 精誠 refined sincerity", ch. 31) and the "fasting of the heart" (心齋 _xinzhai_).

Historical setting: the Daodejing is traditionally attributed to 老子 Laozi, a supposed older contemporary of Confucius (6th c. BCE). Modern scholarship treats both the figure and the attribution as legendary. The text is a layered anthology: bamboo slips from Guodian (郭店, c. 300 BCE) contain about a third of it, and the Mawangdui silk manuscripts (168 BCE) contain the whole. The received text used below is the Wang Bi (王弼, 226-249 CE) edition. Only the "Inner Chapters" (1-7) of the Zhuangzi are usually credited to 莊周 Zhuang Zhou (4th c. BCE); the Outer and Miscellaneous chapters come from later hands, and the received 33-chapter text was edited by Guo Xiang (郭象, d. 312). The Liezi (列子) bears the name of a figure mentioned in the Zhuangzi, but most scholars (following A.C. Graham) date the received text to around the 3rd-4th c. CE. "Philosophical Daoism" (道家 _daojia_) and the organised religion (道教 _daojiao_, from the 2nd c. CE onward, with priests, liturgy and immortality practices) are related but not identical. Daoism remains a living religion, particularly in Taiwan, Hong Kong, the PRC and the diaspora.

Notes on the PD translations:

- **Legge** is accurate but stiff. He fills out the terse Chinese with words in parentheses, which can be dropped for display if marked as an adaptation, and he renders some chapters as rhymed verse (e.g. 52.5 and 58.1).
- **H.A. Giles** reads smoothly but Christianizes: he uses "God" for 天 _tian_ and even for 鬼 _gui_ ("ghosts/spirits", ch. 23), and he translates 天光 "heavenly light" as "divine radiance." It is quotable, but flag this on the page if you display it.

## Candidate passages

All quotations were copied by script from the saved fetched file. Legge's own parentheses are preserved.

### 1. Daodejing ch. 8.1: "The highest excellence is like water"

> 8. 1. The highest excellence is like (that of) water. The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things, and in its occupying, without striving (to the contrary), the low place which all men dislike. Hence (its way) is near to (that of) the Tao.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese (Wang Bi): 上善若水。水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。 (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/道德經_(王弼本))
- Why: the signature Daoist image of humility. Short, concrete and universally loved.

### 2. Daodejing ch. 49.2: Goodness to the not-good

> 2. To those who are good (to me), I am good; and to those who are not good (to me), I am also good;--and thus (all) get to be good. To those who are sincere (with me), I am sincere; and to those who are not sincere (with me), I am also sincere;--and thus (all) get to be sincere.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 善者，吾善之；不善者，吾亦善之，德善。信者，吾信之；不信者，吾亦信之，德信。
- Why: unconditional goodness and sincerity (信). Legge reads 德 as 得 ("thus all get to be good"); many moderns read it as "virtue is goodness."

### 3. Daodejing ch. 52.4-5: "Who uses well his light"

> 4. The perception of what is small is (the secret of) clear-sightedness; the guarding of what is soft and tender is (the secret of) strength.
>
> 5.
>
> Who uses well his light,
> Reverting to its (source so) bright,
> Will from his body ward all blight,
> And hides the unchanging from men's sight.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 見小曰明，守柔曰強。用其光，復歸其明，無遺身殃，是為習常。
- Why: **Strong light resonance.** Light (光) is to be turned back toward its source of clarity (明). Pair it with John 3:21.

### 4. Daodejing ch. 58.3: "Bright, but does not dazzle"

> 3. Therefore the sage is (like) a square which cuts no one (with its angles); (like) a corner which injures no one (with its sharpness). He is straightforward, but allows himself no license; he is bright, but does not dazzle.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 是以聖人方而不割，廉而不劌，直而不肆，光而不燿。
- Why: an image of integrity without harshness. "Bright, but does not dazzle" (光而不燿) is a lovely counterpoint to "come to the light."

### 5. Daodejing ch. 63.1: "Recompense injury with kindness"

> 63. 1. (It is the way of the Tao) to act without (thinking of) acting; to conduct affairs without (feeling the) trouble of them; to taste without discerning any flavour; to consider what is small as great, and a few as many; and to recompense injury with kindness.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 為無為，事無事，味無味。大小多少，報怨以德。
- Why: 報怨以德 is the famous phrase that Confucius explicitly disputes in Analects 14 (see Bridges).

### 6. Daodejing ch. 67.2-3: The three treasures

> 2. But I have three precious things which I prize and hold fast. The first is gentleness; the second is economy; and the third is shrinking from taking precedence of others.
>
> 3. With that gentleness I can be bold; with that economy I can be liberal; shrinking from taking precedence of others, I can become a vessel of the highest honour.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 我有三寶，持而保之。一曰慈，二曰儉，三曰不敢為天下先。慈故能勇，儉故能廣，不敢為天下先，故能成器長。
- Why: the closest thing Daoism has to a short list of virtues. 慈 is often translated "compassion" or "love".

### 7. Daodejing ch. 81.1-2: "Sincere words are not fine"

> 81. 1. Sincere words are not fine; fine words are not sincere. Those who are skilled (in the Tao) do not dispute (about it); the disputatious are not skilled in it. Those who know (the Tao) are not extensively learned; the extensively learned do not know it.
> 82. The sage does not accumulate (for himself). The more that he expends for others, the more does he possess of his own; the more that he gives to others, the more does he have himself.

- Translation: Legge 1891. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt
- Chinese: 信言不美，美言不信。…聖人不積，既以為人己愈有，既以與人己愈多。
- Why: truthfulness over eloquence, and generosity that enriches the giver. Section 2 alone stands well too.

### 8. Zhuangzi ch. 23 (庚桑楚 Gengsang Chu): "A divine radiance"

> "Those whose hearts are in a state of repose give forth a divine radiance, by the light of which they see themselves as they are. And only by cultivating such repose can man attain to the constant.

- Translation: H.A. Giles 1889. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt
- Chinese: 宇泰定者，發乎天光。發乎天光者，人見其人，人有脩者，乃今有恆。 (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/庚桑楚)
- Why: **Strongest light passage in the corpus.** A calm heart gives off "heavenly light" (天光), in which a person is seen as he truly is. Giles' "divine radiance" stands for 天光 "heaven's light."

### 9. Zhuangzi ch. 23 (庚桑楚): Evil done in the light and in secret

> "Whatsoever is not said in all sincerity, is wrongly said. And not to be able to rid oneself of this vice is only to sink deeper towards perdition.
>
> "Those who do evil in the open light of day,--men will punish them. Those who do evil in secret,--God will punish them. Who fears both man and God, he is fit to walk alone.

- Translation: H.A. Giles 1889. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt
- Chinese: 爲不善乎顯明之中者，人得而誅之；爲不善乎幽閒之中者，鬼得而誅之。明乎人，明乎鬼者，然後能獨行。
- Why: **Direct parallel to John 3:20** (evil-doers and concealment). **Caution:** "God" here translates 鬼 _gui_ (ghosts or spirits). Giles' own footnote concedes the term "means strictly those 'spirits'". If you display it, note this or prefer a bracketed "[the spirits]" adaptation. "Fit to walk alone" (獨行) means able to act with integrity unobserved.

### 10. Zhuangzi ch. 31 (漁父 The Old Fisherman): The man who fled his shadow, and "truth unalloyed"

> "There was once a man who was so afraid of his shadow and so disliked his own footsteps that he determined to run away from them. But the oftener he raised his feet the more footsteps he made, and though he ran very hard his shadow never left him. From this he inferred that he went too slowly, and ran as hard as he could without resting, the consequence being that his strength broke down and he died. He was not aware that by going into the shade he would have got rid of his shadow, and that by keeping still he would have put an end to his footsteps. Fool that he was!

> "Our original purity," replied the fisherman, "is the perfection of truth unalloyed. Without this, we cannot influence others. Hence, those who weep to order, though they mourn, do not grieve. Those who assume anger, though violent, do not inspire awe. Those who affect friendship, though they smile, are not in unison."

- Translation: H.A. Giles 1889. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt
- Chinese: 人有畏影惡迹而去之走者，舉足愈數而迹愈多，走愈疾而影不離身，自以爲尚遲，疾走不休，絕力而死。不知處陰以休影，處靜以息迹，愚亦甚矣！ and 眞者，精誠之至也。不精不誠，不能動人。故强哭者，雖悲，不哀。强怒者，雖嚴，不威。强親者，雖笑，不和。 (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/漁父)
- Why: two separable gems. The shadow parable is a striking light-and-shadow image with an un-Johannine moral (stillness, not striving). The second defines 真 (genuineness) as "the perfection of refined sincerity" (精誠之至). Giles renders 眞 as "original purity."
- Note: the Outer/Miscellaneous chapters are not attributed to Zhuang Zhou himself.

### 11. Zhuangzi ch. 32 (列禦寇 Lie Yukou): Zhuangzi's own death

> When Chuang Tzŭ was about to die, his disciples expressed a wish to give him a splendid funeral. But Chuang Tzŭ said, "With Heaven and Earth for my coffin and shell; with the sun, moon, and stars as my burial regalia; and with all creation to escort me to the grave,--are not my funeral paraphernalia ready to hand?"

- Translation: H.A. Giles 1889. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt
- Chinese: 莊子將死，弟子欲厚葬之。莊子曰：「吾以天地爲棺槨，以日月爲連璧，星辰爲珠璣，萬物爲齎送。吾葬具豈不備邪？何以加此？」 (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/莊子/列禦寇)
- Why: a celebrated, self-contained death passage with cosmic light imagery (sun, moon and stars).

### 12. Liezi ch. 2 (黃帝 The Yellow Emperor): The man the sea-gulls trusted

> There was once a man, a sailor by profession, who was very fond of sea-gulls. Every morning he went into the sea and swam about in their midst, at which times a hundred gulls and more would constantly flock about him.
>
> One day his father said to him: 'I am told that sea-gulls swim about with you in the water. I wish you would catch one or two for me to make pets of.' On the following day, the sailor went down to the sea as usual, but lo! the gulls only wheeled about in the air and would not alight.

- Translation: Lionel Giles 1912. Source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Taoist_teachings_from_the_book_of_Lieh_Tz%C5%AD/Book_2
- Chinese: 海上之人有好漚鳥者，每旦之海上，從漚鳥游，漚鳥之至者百住而不止。其父曰：「吾聞漚鳥皆從汝游，汝取來，吾玩之。」明日之海上，漚鳥舞而不下也。 (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/列子/黃帝篇)
- Why: a parable of sincerity. Hidden intent shows itself, and creatures sense a divided heart. The Chinese continues 至言去言，至爲無爲 ("perfect speech is without speech, perfect action is without action").

## Bridges

1. **Light and concealment: John 3:19-21 ↔ Zhuangzi 23.** Both say wrongdoing cannot really hide. Zhuangzi: "those who do evil in secret, the spirits punish them," and a settled heart "emits heavenly light, in which a man is seen as he is." The difference: John's light is a person whom evildoers avoid. Zhuangzi's light is the natural radiance of an unagitated heart, and the remedy is stillness rather than coming forward.
2. **Recompensing injury: Daodejing 63 (報怨以德) ↔ Analects XIV.36 (Legge's numbering; XIV.34 in some editions) ↔ Matthew 5:44 / Romans 12:20-21.** Someone asks Confucius about returning kindness for injury, and he replies (Legge, verified at https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4094/pg4094.txt): "'With what then will you recompense kindness? … Recompense injury with justice, and recompense kindness with kindness.'" The word rendered "justice" is 直, "uprightness". This is one of the clearest intra-Chinese tensions. The Tirukkural (314) and Jesus side with Laozi.
3. **Sincerity as the root: Zhuangzi 31 (眞者，精誠之至也) and Daodejing 81 ↔ Doctrine of the Mean 中庸 ch. 20/25 (誠 _cheng_, "sincerity is the way of Heaven") ↔ Shinto _makoto_ (誠) ↔ Kural 298-299 (truth as inner purity and as the only true lamp).** Daoism shares the vocabulary of 誠 with Confucianism, but locates it in the unforced rather than the cultivated.
4. **Humility and water: Daodejing 8 and 78 ↔ the Beatitudes (the meek, Matthew 5:5) ↔ Philippians 2:5-8 (self-emptying).** Daodejing 78's sage "who bears men's direful woes" and is hailed as king has often been compared with Isaiah 53's suffering servant. That comparison is the reader's own, not a historical link.
5. **Death without dread: Zhuangzi 18 and 32, Liezi 1 ↔ Socrates in Plato's _Phaedo_ ↔ Ecclesiastes 3:1-2 ↔ Buddhist _anicca_ (impermanence).** Tension: for Zhuangzi death is a seasonal transformation (化) to be accepted, not a passage to judgment or liberation.
6. **The unconditional good: Daodejing 49 ↔ Matthew 5:45 (sun and rain given to evil and good alike) ↔ Dhammapada 5 (hatred is not ceased by hatred).**

## Beware (misattributions and pitfalls)

- **"When I let go of what I am, I become what I might be"**: not in the Daodejing. A modern self-help line.
- **"Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished"**: a loose modern paraphrase. There is no such line in the Daodejing.
- **"Care about what other people think and you will always be their prisoner"**: not found in the Daodejing. Popularised via modern "versions."
- **"If you are depressed you are living in the past. If you are anxious you are living in the future…"**: fake.
- **"Be like water"** as a Laozi quote: the phrasing is Bruce Lee's (1971), although it is inspired by chapters 8 and 78.
- **"The journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step"** is genuine (ch. 64), but the Chinese says 千里之行，始於足下, "begins beneath one's feet." Legge has "the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step." Cite the chapter.
- **"Mastering others is strength; mastering yourself is true power"**: this wording is Stephen Mitchell's 1988 rendering of ch. 33, which is not a translation and is in copyright. Use Legge.
- **Mitchell and Le Guin "versions"** are the main source of most viral "Lao Tzu" quotes. Neither knew classical Chinese, and both are copyrighted.
- **Giles' "God"** in the Zhuangzi translates 天 (Heaven), 鬼 (spirits) and similar terms. Don't present it as Daoist monotheism.
- **Historicity**: don't present Laozi as a securely historical author, or the whole Zhuangzi as Zhuang Zhou's own writing. The Liezi is probably 3rd-4th c. CE.
- **ctext.org scraping**: forbidden by its terms, and scraped text may be deliberately corrupted. Verify any Chinese by hand on ctext before display.
