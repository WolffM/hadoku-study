# Buddhism

## Overview

The Buddha (Pali _Buddha_, "awakened one"), Siddhattha Gotama, taught in the Ganges basin of north India, probably in the 5th century BCE. Scholars dispute the exact dates; c. 480–400 BCE is a common estimate. His ethical vision rests on a diagnosis. Suffering (_dukkha_ दुक्ख) comes from craving (_taṇhā_) and ignorance (_avijjā_), and it can end (_nibbāna_ / Skt. _nirvāṇa_ निर्वाण) by following the Noble Eightfold Path. Ethics (_sīla_ सील, "virtue/conduct") is one of the path's three trainings, alongside concentration (_samādhi_) and wisdom (_paññā_). It is not a set of commands from a god. It is the practical ground of a mind that sees clearly. The moral law of action and result is _kamma_ (Skt. _karma_ कर्म): intention (_cetanā_) is the deed, and what follows a deed follows from the quality of the mind behind it (Dhammapada 1–2 below).

The tradition's key moral terms are these:

- **non-harming**: _ahiṃsā_ / _avihiṃsā_
- **the four "divine abidings"** (_brahmavihāra_): loving-kindness (_mettā_ मेत्ता), compassion (_karuṇā_ करुणा), sympathetic joy (_muditā_) and equanimity (_upekkhā_)
- **heedfulness**: _appamāda_, the Buddha's last word
- **the Five Precepts** (_pañcasīla_): refrain from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech and intoxicants

Light imagery is pervasive. Ignorance is darkness (_andhakāra_), and wisdom is a lamp (_padīpa_) or the moon coming out from clouds. That makes the tradition a rich partner for John 3:19–21. Because the Pali _dīpa_ can mean both "island" and "lamp", one famous saying has two English forms (see DN 16 below).

After the Buddha's death the teaching was preserved orally and written down in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BCE in the Pali Canon (_Tipiṭaka_) of the Theravāda. Parallel collections survive in Sanskrit, Gāndhārī, Chinese and Tibetan. The Mahāyāna movement (from c. 1st century BCE) recentred the ideal on the _bodhisattva_, who vows to free all beings. Its great ethical poem is Śāntideva's _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ (बोधिचर्यावतार, "Entering the Path of Awakening", Nālandā, early 8th century CE). Buddhism is a living religion of several hundred million people. Theravāda in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, Mahāyāna in East Asia and Vajrayāna in the Himalayas each have their own canons and emphases, so passages should be attributed to their specific text, not to "the Buddha" in general.

## Candidate passages

### 1. Dhammapada 1–2: the mind leads

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt (Ch. 1, "The Twin Verses")

> 1. All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made up of our thoughts. If a man speaks or acts with an evil thought, pain follows him, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that draws the carriage.
>
> 2. All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made up of our thoughts. If a man speaks or acts with a pure thought, happiness follows him, like a shadow that never leaves him.

- **Pali (v. 1):** _Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; Manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā; Tato naṁ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṁva vahato padaṁ._ (https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/pli/ms)
- **Sujato (CC0), v. 1:** "Intention is the leader of things; intention is first, they're made by intention. If with corrupt intent you speak or act, suffering follows you, like a wheel, the ox's foot." (https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/en/sujato)
- **Why:** These are the opening verses of the most-read Buddhist text: inner intention shapes outer life. Müller's "All that we are is the result of what we have thought" is an expansive rendering. Sujato's version is closer to the Pali.

### 2. Dhammapada 5: hatred does not cease by hatred

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt

> 5. For hatred does not cease by hatred at any time: hatred ceases by love, this is an old rule.

- **Pali:** _Na hi verena verāni, sammantīdha kudācanaṁ; Averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano._
- **Sujato (CC0):** "For never is hatred laid to rest by hate, it's laid to rest by love: this is an ancient teaching." (https://suttacentral.net/dhp1-20/en/sujato)
- **Why:** This is perhaps the most universal Buddhist ethical saying. _esa dhammo sanantano_ ("this is an eternal law") presents it as a law of nature, not a sectarian rule.

### 3. Dhammapada 129: all fear death; treat others like yourself (golden rule)

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt (Ch. 10, "Punishment")

> 129. All men tremble at punishment, all men fear death; remember that you are like unto them, and do not kill, nor cause slaughter.

- **Pali:** _Sabbe tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbe bhāyanti maccuno; Attānaṁ upamaṁ katvā, na haneyya na ghātaye._ (https://suttacentral.net/dhp129-145/en/sujato)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "All tremble at the rod, all fear death. Treating others like oneself, neither kill nor incite to kill."
- **Why:** This is Buddhism's golden rule. _attānaṁ upamaṁ katvā_ means "making oneself the comparison". It grounds non-violence in empathy.

### 4. Dhammapada 146: "Why do you not seek a light?" (light/darkness, strongest John 3 resonance)

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt (Ch. 11, "Old Age")

> 146. How is there laughter, how is there joy, as this world is always burning? Why do you not seek a light, ye who are surrounded by darkness?

- **Pali:** _Ko nu hāso kimānando, niccaṁ pajjalite sati; Andhakārena onaddhā, padīpaṁ na gavesatha._ (https://suttacentral.net/dhp146-156/en/sujato)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "What is joy, what is laughter, when the flames are ever burning? Swathed in darkness, would you not seek a light?"
- **Why:** This pairs naturally with John 3:19: darkness is the human default, and the moral question is whether one goes looking for the lamp.

### 5. Dhammapada 183: the teaching of all Buddhas in one verse

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt (Ch. 14, "The Buddha")

> 183. Not to commit any sin, to do good, and to purify one's mind, that is the teaching of (all) the Awakened.

- **Pali:** _Sabbapāpassa akaraṇaṁ, kusalassa upasampadā; Sacittapariyodapanaṁ, etaṁ buddhāna sāsanaṁ._ (https://suttacentral.net/dhp179-196/en/sujato)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "Not to do any evil; to embrace the good; to purify one's mind: this is the instruction of the Buddhas."
- **Why:** This is the classic one-verse summary of Buddhist ethics (the _Ovāda Pātimokkha_), recited across all schools. _pariyodapana_ is literally "making bright/clean".

### 6. Dhammapada 223: overcome evil by good

- **Translation:** Müller (1881)
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2017/pg2017.txt (Ch. 17, "Anger")

> 223. Let a man overcome anger by love, let him overcome evil by good; let him overcome the greedy by liberality, the liar by truth!

- **Pali:** _Akkodhena jine kodhaṁ, asādhuṁ sādhunā jine; Jine kadariyaṁ dānena, saccenālikavādinaṁ._ (https://suttacentral.net/dhp221-234/en/sujato)
- **Why:** This is a near-verbatim parallel to Romans 12:21, and it ends on truth (_sacca_) overcoming the liar.

### 7. Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.131: three things shine in the open (hidden vs. manifest, strong John 3:20–21 resonance)

- **Translation:** Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0)
- **Source:** https://suttacentral.net/an3.131/en/sujato (fetched via https://suttacentral.net/api/bilarasuttas/an3.131/sujato)

> "Mendicants, three things are conveyed under cover, not in the open. What three? Ladies are conveyed for marriage with a veil, not unveiled. Hymns of the brahmins are conveyed under cover, not openly. Wrong view is conveyed under cover, not in the open. … Three things dazzle in the open, not under cover. What three? The moon dazzles in the open, not under cover. The sun dazzles in the open, not under cover. The teaching and training proclaimed by a Realized One dazzle in the open, not under cover."

- **Pali (second half):** _Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, vivaṭāni virocanti, no paṭicchannāni. … Candamaṇḍalaṁ … sūriyamaṇḍalaṁ … tathāgatappavedito dhammavinayo, bhikkhave, vivaṭo virocati, no paṭicchanno._
- **Why:** This is the source of the popular (garbled) "Three things cannot be long hidden: the sun, the moon, and the truth" (see Beware). The genuine text is closer to John 3:20–21 than the meme. Wrong view works under cover, while the true teaching shines in the open. For display, the second half alone ("Three things dazzle in the open…") is self-contained and avoids the dated first item.

### 8. Sutta Nipāta 1.8, Metta Sutta vv. 7–8: as a mother protects her only child

- **Translation:** V. Fausbøll, SBE X pt. 2 (1881), Mettasutta vv. 7–8 (his numbering 148–149; standard Sn 149–150)
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/wg910/WG910-1881%20-The%20Sacred%20Books%20of%20East%20-%20Vol%2010%20of%2050-Buddhism-Dhammapada%20And%20Sutta%20Nipata_djvu.txt (OCR scan; spacing and line-break hyphens normalized, no words changed)

> 7. As a mother at the risk of her life watches over her own child, her only child, so also let every one cultivate a boundless (friendly) mind towards all beings.
>
> 8. And let him cultivate goodwill towards all the world, a boundless (friendly) mind, above and below and across, unobstructed, without hatred, without enmity.

- **Before publishing:** the text comes from OCR, so check it against the page image of the scan.
- **Pali:** _Mātā yathā niyaṁ puttam, āyusā ekaputtamanurakkhe; Evampi sabbabhūtesu, mānasaṁ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṁ. Mettañca sabbalokasmi, mānasaṁ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṁ; Uddhaṁ adho ca tiriyañca, asambādhaṁ averamasapattaṁ._ (https://suttacentral.net/snp1.8/pli/ms)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "Even as a mother would protect with her life her child, her only child, so too for all creatures unfold a boundless heart. With love for the whole world, unfold a boundless heart: above, below, all round, unconstricted, without enmity or foe." (https://suttacentral.net/snp1.8/en/sujato)
- **Why:** This is the heart of the most-chanted Theravāda text. Sujato's rendering is probably the best for display: lyrical, accurate and CC0.

### 9. Dīgha Nikāya 16 (Mahāparinibbāna Sutta) II.33: "be ye lamps unto yourselves"

- **Translation:** T. W. Rhys Davids, SBE XI (1881), ch. II §33
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/buddhistsuttas00daviuoft/buddhistsuttas00daviuoft_djvu.txt (OCR; spacing normalized)

> 'Therefore, O Ananda, be ye lamps unto yourselves. Be ye a refuge to yourselves. Betake yourselves to no external refuge. Hold fast to the truth as a lamp. Hold fast as a refuge to the truth. Look not for refuge to any one besides yourselves.

- **Pali:** _Tasmātihānanda, attadīpā viharatha attasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā, dhammadīpā dhammasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā._ (https://suttacentral.net/dn16/pli/ms, segment 2.26.1)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "So Ānanda, live as your own island, your own refuge, with no other refuge. Let the teaching be your island and your refuge, with no other refuge."
- **Provenance note:** "Be a lamp unto yourselves" is a genuine translation, Rhys Davids 1881, but contested. Pali _dīpa_ = Skt. _dvīpa_ "island" or _dīpa_ "lamp". Most modern scholars (and the commentary's flood imagery) favour "island". Dhp 236 has the same ambiguity: Müller renders _karohi dīpamattano_ as "Make thyself an island". The lamp reading is still defensible and widely loved. If used, attribute it to Rhys Davids' translation and keep the "Hold fast to the truth as a lamp" clause, which gives the light/truth resonance with John 3:21.

### 10. Dīgha Nikāya 16 VI.10: the Buddha's last words (death)

- **Translation:** Rhys Davids, SBE XI (1881), ch. VI §10
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/buddhistsuttas00daviuoft/buddhistsuttas00daviuoft_djvu.txt

> Then the Blessed One addressed the brethren, and said: 'Behold now, brethren, I exhort you, saying, "Decay is inherent in all component things! Work out your salvation with diligence!"' This was the last word of the Tathagata!

- **Pali:** _vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādetha._ (DN 16 segment 3.51.4 in SuttaCentral's numbering)
- **Sujato (CC0):** "'Conditions fall apart. Persist with diligence.'"
- **Why:** This is the canonical deathbed teaching. Rhys Davids' "Work out your salvation" echoes Philippians 2:12, which is worth noting as a translator's choice, not a source parallel.

### 11. Śāntideva, _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ III (the bodhisattva vow): "a lamp for them who need a lamp"

- **Translation:** L. D. Barnett, _The Path of Light_ (1909), ch. III "Taking the Thought of Enlightenment" (≈ BCA III.17–18; Barnett gives no verse numbers)
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/pathoflight00santiala/pathoflight00santiala_djvu.txt (OCR; spacing and line-break hyphens normalized)

> I would be a protector of the unprotected, a guide of wayfarers, a ship, a dyke, and a bridge for them who seek the further Shore; a lamp for them who need a lamp, a bed for them who need a bed, a slave for all beings who need a slave.

- **Why:** This is the most beloved Mahāyāna vow of service, with explicit lamp imagery. Its theme is humility: becoming whatever others need.

### 12. Śāntideva, _Bodhicaryāvatāra_ VIII (exchange of self and others)

- **Translation:** Barnett (1909), ch. VIII "The Perfect Contemplation" (≈ BCA VIII.90–94; Barnett marks his own ellipsis)
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/pathoflight00santiala/pathoflight00santiala_djvu.txt

> First he will diligently foster the thought that his fellow-creatures are the same as himself. "All have the same sorrows, the same joys as I, and I must guard them like myself. … I must destroy the pain of another as though it were my own, because it is a pain; I must show kindness to others, for they are creatures as I am myself.

- **Why:** This is the Mahāyāna's philosophical grounding of the golden rule ("equalizing self and others").

## Bridges

1. **Light sought vs. darkness loved.** Dhp 146 ("Why do you not seek a light, ye who are surrounded by darkness?") and AN 3.131 (wrong view travels under cover, while the Dhamma "dazzles in the open") pair with **Christianity, John 3:19–21**: deeds that hate the light vs. truth that comes to the light. The tension is that in Buddhism the darkness is ignorance (_avijjā_) more than willful evil.
2. **The golden rule.** Dhp 129 ("remember that you are like unto them") parallels **Confucianism, Analects 15:24** (己所不欲，勿施於人), **Hillel (Talmud, Shabbat 31a)** and **Matthew 7:12**. Śāntideva VIII extends it from reciprocity to exchanging self and other.
3. **Overcoming evil with good.** Dhp 5 and 223 parallel **Romans 12:21** ("Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good") and **Daodejing 63** (報怨以德, "repay injury with virtue"). Contrast **Analects 14:34**, where Confucius rejects repaying injury with virtue: "repay injury with straightness" (以直報怨).
4. **The mote and the beam (humility).** Dhp 252 in Müller: "The fault of others is easily perceived, but that of oneself is difficult to perceive; a man winnows his neighbour's faults like chaff, but his own fault he hides…" This is near-identical to **Matthew 7:3–5**. The **Platform Sutra**'s formless verse ("He who treads the Path in earnest / Sees not the mistakes of the world") is the Zen descendant.
5. **Non-violence.** The Metta Sutta and Dhp 129–130 share common roots with **Jainism**'s _ahiṃsā_ (Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.4.1: all beings should not be slain) and later inform **Gandhi**'s reading of the **Bhagavad Gita**. The tension: the Gita (ch. 2) urges Arjuna to fight.
6. **Death and diligence.** DN 16's last words ("Decay is inherent in all component things!") parallel **Psalm 90:12** ("teach us to number our days"), **Stoic** _memento mori_ and **Hagakure**'s daily meditation on death. Buddhism's distinctive turn is that impermanence is a reason for diligence (_appamāda_), not dread.

## Beware (misattributions and garbles)

- **"Three things cannot be long hidden: the sun, the moon, and the truth."** This garbles AN 3.131 (see #7). The sutta says three things _shine in the open_ (moon, sun, the Buddha's teaching) and pairs them with three things that travel under cover.
- **"Thousands of candles can be lighted from a single candle, and the life of the candle will not be shortened. Happiness never decreases by being shared."** This is a modern paraphrase. The torch image does exist in the _Sutra in Forty-two Sections_ (四十二章經, Taishō 784 recension: 譬如一炬之火數千百人。各以炬來分取。熟食除冥。此炬如故。 "Like the flame of one torch from which hundreds and thousands take light with their own torches… it dispels darkness, and the torch is as before." That gloss is the researcher's, not a published translation; source https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/四十二章經_(大正新脩大藏經譯本)). The image is absent from the older Korean-canon recension, and the popular wording is not a translation.
- **"Holding on to anger is like grasping a hot coal with the intent of throwing it at someone else; you are the one who gets burned."** Not in the suttas. It is usually traced to a loose paraphrase of Buddhaghosa's _Visuddhimagga_ (5th c. CE).
- **"What we think, we become" / "The mind is everything. What you think you become."** These are inflated paraphrases of Dhp 1–2. Use the real verses.
- **"Believe nothing, no matter where you read it or who said it… unless it agrees with your own reason."** This distorts the _Kālāma Sutta_ (AN 3.65), which also asks one to consider what "the wise praise".
- **"Peace comes from within. Do not seek it without."** and **"You yourself, as much as anybody in the entire universe, deserve your love and affection."** Neither is canonical. The second is a paraphrase by the modern teacher Sharon Salzberg.
- **"Be a lamp unto yourself"** is genuine (Rhys Davids' DN 16), but note the island/lamp ambiguity (#9) and attribute the translation.
