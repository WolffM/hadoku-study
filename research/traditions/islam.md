# Islam (Qur'an and Hadith)

## Overview

Islam traces itself to the revelations received by the Prophet Muhammad (c. 570-632 CE) in Mecca and Medina over some twenty-three years and gathered in the **Qur'an** ("recitation"). Muslims understand it as the speech of God (Allah) in Arabic. Its ethics are framed by **tawhid** (the oneness of God) and by the human being's role as **khalifa** (steward/vicegerent) on earth and as **'abd** (servant) of God. Every deed is weighed on a Day of Reckoning, and the transience of worldly life (**dunya**) is a constant theme. Key ethical terms: **taqwa** (God-consciousness, mindful reverence), **'adl** and **qist** (justice, equity), **ihsan** (doing what is beautiful/excellent), **birr** (righteousness, goodness), **rahma** (mercy; God is _al-Rahman al-Rahim_), **sidq** (truthfulness), **sadaqa** (voluntary charity) and **zakat** (obligatory alms), **niyya** (intention), **sabr** (patience, endurance), **haqq** (truth, right), and **nur** (light). God is "the Light of the heavens and the earth" (24:35).

The **Hadith** are reports of the Prophet's words and deeds transmitted through chains of narrators (**isnad**). They were compiled and graded for reliability in the 8th-9th centuries, above all in the "Six Books", headed by the _Sahih_ collections of al-Bukhari (d. 870) and Muslim (d. 875). Together with the Qur'an they form the **Sunna**, the normative model of life. Imam al-Nawawi's _Forty Hadith_ (_al-Arba'un_, 13th century) is the classic short anthology of ethical-spiritual hadith, memorized across the Muslim world. It begins with "Actions are only by intentions".

Qur'anic ethics are notably social. True righteousness is not ritual orientation but giving to kin, orphans, the poor and the traveller, keeping one's word, and endurance (2:177). Justice must be upheld even against oneself or one's family (4:135), and human diversity exists "that ye may know one another" (49:13). Ihsan is defined in the Hadith of Gabriel as worshipping God "as though you see Him", an ethic of living in full view.

## Candidate passages

Normalization: Pickthall is copied from Project Gutenberg #16955 (a side-by-side file of Yusuf Ali, Pickthall and Shakir; only the "P:" lines are used), with line breaks collapsed. Gutenberg notes the file was "re-proofed and corrected ... against paper copies"; one typo was spotted (103:2 "man is a state of loss", surely "in a state"), so spot-check against a scan of the 1930 edition before display. Abu'l-Fadl is copied from two independent archive.org OCR scans of the 1924 edition, and the wording agrees across them. ### 1. Qur'an 24:35 (Ayat al-Nur, the Light Verse)

> Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The similitude of His light is as a niche wherein is a lamp. The lamp is in a glass. The glass is as it were a shining star. (This lamp is) kindled from a blessed tree, an olive neither of the East nor of the West, whose oil would almost glow forth (of itself) though no fire touched it. Light upon light. Allah guideth unto His light whom He will. And Allah speaketh to mankind in allegories, for Allah is Knower of all things.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt. Rodwell 1861 also verified at https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2800/pg2800.txt: "God is the LIGHT of the Heavens and of the Earth. His Light is like a niche in which is a lamp-the lamp encased in glass-the glass, as it were, a glistening star. ..."
- **Why:** The great light text of Islam, central to al-Ghazali's _Mishkat al-Anwar_ and to Sufism. It sits naturally beside John 3:19-21 and 1 John 1:5. Long; "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth ... Light upon light" can be excerpted.

### 2. Qur'an 2:177 (what righteousness really is)

> It is not righteousness that ye turn your faces to the East and the West; but righteous is he who believeth in Allah and the Last Day and the angels and the Scripture and the prophets; and giveth wealth, for love of Him, to kinsfolk and to orphans and the needy and the wayfarer and to those who ask, and to set slaves free; and observeth proper worship and payeth the poor-due. And those who keep their treaty when they make one, and the patient in tribulation and adversity and time of stress. Such are they who are sincere. Such are the Allah-fearing.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** The Qur'an's own "Micah 6:8 / James 1:27": birr is faith enacted in generosity, fidelity and endurance, not orientation in ritual.

### 3. Qur'an 2:271 (giving openly or in secret)

> If ye publish your almsgiving, it is well, but if ye hide it and give it to the poor, it will be better for you, and will atone for some of your ill-deeds. Allah is Informed of what ye do.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** An almost exact counterpart to Matthew 6:1-4 and 5:16 held together: open giving is good, hidden giving better. A direct bridge for the "doing good in the open" theme.

### 4. Qur'an 4:135 (justice even against oneself)

> O ye who believe! Be ye staunch in justice, witnesses for Allah, even though it be against yourselves or (your) parents or (your) kindred, whether (the case be of) a rich man or a poor man, for Allah is nearer unto both (them ye are). So follow not passion lest ye lapse (from truth) and if ye lapse or fall away, then lo! Allah is ever Informed of what ye do.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** Truth-telling and justice that cost the speaker. Strong on truth. Shorter sibling, also verified: 5:8, "let not hatred of any people seduce you that ye deal not justly. Deal justly, that is nearer to your duty."

### 5. Qur'an 49:13 (nations and tribes)

> O mankind! Lo! We have created you male and female, and have made you nations and tribes that ye may know one another. Lo! the noblest of you, in the sight of Allah, is the best in conduct. Lo! Allah is Knower, Aware.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD). Note: "best in conduct" renders _atqakum_ ("most God-conscious", taqwa). Rodwell 1861 (verified, same Gutenberg #2800 file) has "he who feareth Him most".
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** Human diversity as a reason for mutual knowledge, with nobility by conduct not lineage. Echoes Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5's "no man would say ... my ancestors are greater than yours".

### 6. Qur'an 41:34 (repel evil with what is better)

> The good deed and the evil deed are not alike. Repel the evil deed with one which is better, then lo! he, between whom and thee there was enmity (will become) as though he was a bosom friend.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** Non-retaliation that transforms the enemy. The twin of Romans 12:21 and Proverbs 25:21-22.

### 7. Qur'an 5:32 (one life as all humankind)

> For that cause We decreed for the Children of Israel that whosoever killeth a human being for other than manslaughter or corruption in the earth, it shall be as if he had killed all mankind, and whoso saveth the life of one, it shall be as if he had saved the life of all mankind. Our messengers came unto them of old with clear proofs (of Allah's Sovereignty), but afterwards lo! many of them became prodigals in the earth.

- **Translation:** Pickthall, 1930 (US PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16955/pg16955.txt
- **Why:** An explicit inter-scriptural bridge: the Qur'an cites a decree given to the Children of Israel, matching Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5. **Present it with its full wording**, including the exception clause and the addressee, rather than the popular truncated slogan.

### 8. Hadith: the golden rule (Bukhari, Muslim; Nawawi's Forty no. 13)

> By Him in whose hand stands my life! no servant (of God really) believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.

- **Translation:** Mirza Abu'l-Fadl, _Sayings of the Prophet Muhammad_ (1924), [214]; narrator Anas; collections BU (Bukhari), MU (Muslim), TI (Tirmidhi), NA (Nasa'i). US PD.
- **Source URL:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.272543/2015.272543.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt (cross-checked: https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.215961/2015.215961.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt)
- **Why:** Islam's golden rule. Some versions of the Arabic (e.g. in Muslim and Ahmad) read "for his neighbour", a point of commentary on whether "brother" means fellow believer or fellow human.

### 9. Hadith: righteousness and sin (Muslim, Tirmidhi; Nawawi's Forty no. 27)

> I asked the Prophet concerning righteousness and sin. He said, 'Righteousness is to have a good disposition; and sin is what pricks within thy bosom, and what thou wouldst be averse to make public.'

- **Translation:** Abu'l-Fadl (1924), [175]; narrator al-Nawwas b. Sam'an; MU, TI. US PD. (OCR fix: scan 1 "wifc.hm", scan 2 "within".)
- **Source URL:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.215961/2015.215961.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt (and the .272543 scan)
- **Why:** Perhaps the single best cross-tradition companion to John 3:20. Sin is what you would not want brought into the open. Strongly recommended.

### 10. Hadith: actions and intentions (Bukhari, Muslim; Nawawi's Forty no. 1)

> Actions are only (judged) by intentions.

- **Translation:** Abu'l-Fadl (1924), [347]; narrator 'Umar; BU, MU, AB (Abu Dawud), TI, NA. US PD.
- **Source URL:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.272543/2015.272543.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt
- **Why:** The opening hadith of al-Nawawi and of Bukhari's _Sahih_. The inner root of action, parallel to Augustine's "let the root of love be within".

### 11. Hadith: the stranger and the wayfarer (Bukhari; Nawawi's Forty no. 40)

> The Prophet seized me (by the hand), and said, 'Be thou in this world as though thou wert a stranger, or a passer-by on the road, and count thyself among the people of the graves.'

- **Translation:** Abu'l-Fadl (1924), [1080]; narrator Ibn 'Umar; BU. US PD. (OCR fixes: "peop le" → "people", closing quote mark.) A shorter variant, [1073], is also in the scan: "Be thou in this world as though thou wert a stranger, or a traveller on the road."
- **Source URL:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.215961/2015.215961.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt
- **Why:** A memento mori with tenderness: the world as a road, not a home. Bridges to Ecclesiastes, Stoicism and Buddhism.

### 12. Hadith: true strength (Bukhari, Muslim)

> He is not strong or powerful who throws people down, but he is the strong among us who masters himself when angry.

- **Translation:** Abu'l-Fadl (1924), [28]; narrator Abu Hurayrah; BU, MU, MA (Malik). US PD. (OCR fix: "hut" → "but".)
- **Source URL:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.272543/2015.272543.Sayings-Of_djvu.txt
- **Why:** Self-mastery as real strength. Almost word-for-word with Ben Zoma (Avot 4:1) and Proverbs 16:32, and congenial to Stoicism.

## Beware

- **"The ink of the scholar is holier than the blood of the martyr"** is graded weak or fabricated by hadith critics. Don't present it as Prophetic.
- **"Seek knowledge even unto China"** is likewise graded very weak or fabricated (al-Bayhaqi himself flagged it).
- **"The greater jihad is the jihad against the self"** has a chain widely judged weak, though the idea is common in Sufi literature. Attribute it carefully.
- **"Whoever kills one person kills all of humanity"** is often quoted from 5:32 with the addressee ("the Children of Israel") and the exception clause dropped. Quote it in full (#7).
- Rumi and other Sufi poets are often quoted as if they were Qur'an or Hadith (and many "Rumi" lines are themselves spurious). Keep the genres distinct.
- Translation phrasing for Hadith varies widely. Don't splice a modern copyrighted wording (e.g. sunnah.com's Ibrahim/Johnson-Davies) into a PD citation.
- Out of respect, avoid displaying Qur'anic text in ways that imply it is "one wisdom quote among many" without attribution as scripture. Label it "Qur'an, Surah X:Y (trans. Pickthall)", and consider noting that translations are understood as interpretations of the meaning, not the Qur'an itself (Pickthall titled his "The _Meaning_ of the Glorious Koran" for this reason).

## Bridges

1. **Light.** Qur'an 24:35 ("Light upon light") with **Christianity**, 1 John 1:5 ("God is light") and John 3:19-21, and **Zoroastrianism**, where Ahura Mazda is associated with light and the sacred fire. Al-Ghazali's _Mishkat al-Anwar_ (Niche of Lights) explicitly develops 24:35 into a philosophy of illumination, a ready bridge to **Sufism** and to Plato's sun/cave imagery (**Greek**).
2. **What would you be averse to make public?** Hadith [175] / Nawawi 27 ("sin is ... what thou wouldst be averse to make public") is a near-exact ethical twin of **Christianity**, John 3:20 ("everyone who does evil hates the light ... lest his works be exposed").
3. **Open and secret charity.** Qur'an 2:271 and 2:264 (charity "only to be seen of men" is voided) with **Christianity**, Matthew 6:1-4 and 5:16, and **Rabbinic** Avot 1:3.
4. **Golden rule.** Nawawi 13 with **Rabbinic**, Hillel (Shabbat 31a), **Christianity**, Matthew 7:12 and Didache 1:2, and **Hebrew Bible**, Leviticus 19:18.
5. **One life = all humanity.** Qur'an 5:32 explicitly cites the teaching found in **Rabbinic**, Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5.
6. **Death and the traveller.** Hadith [1080] and Qur'an 3:185 with **Hebrew Bible**, Ecclesiastes 7:2, and **Stoicism**, Marcus Aurelius's meditations on transience. The tension with **Epicureanism**: Islam makes death the threshold of judgment, while Epicurus argues "death is nothing to us".
