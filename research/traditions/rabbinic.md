# Rabbinic Judaism (Mishnah, Pirkei Avot, Talmud)

## Overview

Rabbinic Judaism took shape after the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE). Its creators were the sages (**chakhamim**), first the **Tannaim** (teachers of the Mishnah, to c. 200 CE) and then the **Amoraim** (expounders, c. 200-500 CE). They rebuilt Jewish life around Torah study, prayer and deeds of kindness rather than sacrifice. The **Mishnah**, redacted c. 200 CE under Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi, codifies the **Oral Torah** (**Torah she-be'al peh**). The **Talmud** (the Babylonian **Bavli**, c. 500-600 CE, and the Jerusalem **Yerushalmi**, c. 400 CE) records centuries of debate on the Mishnah. It interweaves **halakhah** (law, "the way of walking") with **aggadah** (narrative, ethics, lore).

**Pirkei Avot** ("Chapters of the Fathers", tractate _Avot_ of the Mishnah) is the rabbinic ethical anthology par excellence. It is a chain of maxims attributed to sages from Simeon the Just through Hillel, Shammai and Rabbi Akiva, and it is traditionally studied on Sabbath afternoons. Its key concepts include **gemilut chasadim** (bestowal of loving-kindness), **derekh eretz** (decent conduct, "the way of the land"), **teshuvah** (repentance, return), **lishmah** (acting for its own sake, without ulterior reward), **kavod ha-beriyot** (the dignity of persons), **yetzer ha-ra / yetzer ha-tov** (the evil and good inclinations), and **imitatio Dei**: "just as He is merciful, so be you merciful."

The rabbinic ethos is strongly communal and practical: "not learning but doing is the groundwork". It values argument preserved with all its minority opinions. It also insists that each human being, made in God's image, is a whole world.

### 1. Pirkei Avot 1:2 (Simeon the Just)

> Shime'on ha-Çaddiq was of the remnants of the Great Synagogue. He used to say, On three things the world is stayed; on the Thorah, and on the Worship, and on the bestowal of Kindnesses.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Pirkei_Avot.1?version=english|Sayings%20of%20the%20Jewish%20Fathers%20(Pirqe%20Aboth)%20translated%20by%20Charles%20Taylor%20%5B1897%5D (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.2)
- **Why:** The three pillars (Torah, worship, gemilut chasadim) form the rabbinic ethical frame in one sentence.

### 2. Pirkei Avot 1:3 (Antigonus of Sokho)

> Antigonus of Soko received from Shime'on ha-Çaddiq. He used to say, Be not as slaves that minister to the lord with a view to receive recompense; but be as slaves that minister to the lord without a view to receives recompense; and let the fear of Heaven be upon you.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD). Note: "receives" [sic] appears in Sefaria's digitization, probably a transcription error for "receive".
- **Source URL:** same as #1 (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.3)
- **Why:** Goodness for its own sake (lishmah), not for reward. A direct counterpart to Matthew 6:1-4 and to Stoic virtue-as-its-own-reward.

### 3. Pirkei Avot 1:12 (Hillel)

> Hillel and Shammai received from them. Hillel said, Be of the disciples of Aharon; loving peace, and pursuing peace; loving mankind, and bringing them nigh to the Thorah.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD)
- **Source URL:** same as #1 (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.12)
- **Why:** Active peacemaking plus love of all people (beriyot, "creatures"). Pairs with "Blessed are the peacemakers."

### 4. Pirkei Avot 1:14 (Hillel)

> He used to say, If I am not for myself who is for me? and being for my own self what am I? If not now when?

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD). The CC0 Sefaria Community Translation of the same mishnah, verified on the same API: "He [Rabbi Hillel] used to say: If I am not for me, who will be for me? And when I am for myself alone, what am I? And if not now, then when?"
- **Source URL:** same as #1 (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.14)
- **Why:** Three questions balancing self-respect, altruism and urgency. Among the most quoted lines in Jewish ethics, and genuinely self-contained.

### 5. Pirkei Avot 1:17 (Simeon son of Gamaliel)

> Shime'on his son said, All my days I have grown up amongst the wise, and have not found aught good for a man but silence; not learning but doing is the groundwork; and whoso multiplies words occasions sin.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD)
- **Source URL:** same as #1 (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.1.17)
- **Why:** "Not learning but doing", a strong bridge to James 1:22 ("doers of the word") and to Stoic suspicion of mere talk.

### 6. Pirkei Avot 2:4b (Hillel)

> Hillel said, Separate not thyself from the congregation, and trust not in thyself until the day of thy death; and judge not thy friend until thou comest into his place; and say not of a word which may be heard that in the end it shall be heard; and say not, When I have leisure I will study; perchance thou mayest not have leisure.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD). (Taylor numbers this §5; Sefaria places it in segment 2:4. In many printed editions it is 2:4-5.)
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Pirkei_Avot.2?version=english|Sayings%20of%20the%20Jewish%20Fathers%20(Pirqe%20Aboth)%20translated%20by%20Charles%20Taylor%20%5B1897%5D
- **Why:** "Judge not thy friend until thou comest into his place." Humility in judgment; can be excerpted to that clause alone.

### 7. Pirkei Avot 2:5b (Hillel)

> He used to say, No boor is a sinfearer; nor is the vulgar pious; nor is the shamefast apt to learn, nor the passionate to teach; nor is every one that has much traffic wise. And in a place where there are no men endeavour to be a man.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD)
- **Source URL:** same as #6
- **Why:** The final clause ("where there are no men endeavour to be a man", Hebrew _hishtadel lihyot ish_) is a classic call to moral courage when others fail. It can be shown alone.

### 8. Pirkei Avot 2:15-16 (Rabbi Tarfon)

> R. Tarphon said, The day is short, and the task is great, and the workmen are sluggish, and the reward is much, and the Master of the house is urgent.
>
> He said, It is not for thee to finish the work, nor art thou free to desist therefrom; if thou hast learned much Thorah, they give thee much reward; and faithful is the Master of thy work, who will pay thee the reward of thy work, and know that the recompence of the reward of the righteous is for the time to come.

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD)
- **Source URL:** same as #6 (reader view: https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.2.16)
- **Why:** "It is not for thee to finish the work, nor art thou free to desist therefrom." A sustaining word on finitude and obligation; display that first clause.

### 9. Pirkei Avot 4:1 (Ben Zoma), excerpt

> Ben Zoma said, Who is wise? He that learns from every man; for it is said, From all my teachers I gat understanding (Ps. Cxix. 99). 2. Who is mighty? He that subdues his nature; for it is said, He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city (Prov. xvi. 32). 3. Who is rich? He that is contented with his lot; ...

- **Translation:** Charles Taylor, 1897 (PD). Taylor's internal numbers "2." and "3." are kept here because they sit mid-segment; the passage continues ("4. Who is honoured? He that honours mankind...").
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Pirkei_Avot.4?version=english|Sayings%20of%20the%20Jewish%20Fathers%20(Pirqe%20Aboth)%20translated%20by%20Charles%20Taylor%20%5B1897%5D
- **Why:** Redefines wisdom, strength, wealth and honour as inner and relational qualities. Close kin to Stoic self-mastery and the hadith "the strong man masters himself when angry".

### 10. Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 31a (Hillel and the would-be convert)

> Another time a non-Jew came before Shammai and said, "I will convert if you can teach me the entire Torah while I stand on one foot." Shammai pushed the non-Jews aside with the ruler that was in his hand. The non-Jew came before Hillel and Hillel converted him saying, "What is hateful to you, do not do to your neighbor, that is the entire Torah, the rest is just commentary, now go and study."

- **Translation:** Sefaria Community Translation (**CC0**). Double spaces and "non-Jews" [sic] are as fetched. The CC-BY-NC William Davidson rendering, also fetched, reads: "That which is hateful to you do not do to another; that is the entire Torah, and the rest is its interpretation. Go study."
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Shabbat.31a?version=english|Sefaria%20Community%20Translation (segment 6; reader view https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.31a.6)
- **Why:** The negative golden rule plus "go and study": the classic rabbinic summary of Torah and the hub for golden-rule bridges.

### 11. Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5, excerpt (the warning to capital-case witnesses)

> "It was for this reason that man was first created as one person [Adam], to teach you that anyone who destroys a life is considered by Scripture to have destroyed an entire world; and anyone who saves a life is as if he saved an entire world." And also, to promote peace among the creations, that no man would say to his friend, "My ancestors are greater than yours."

- **Translation:** Open Mishnah (Wikisource), **CC-BY-SA**. Attribution and share-alike are required, so flag before use. The CC0 Sefaria Community Translation (also fetched) reads "anyone who destroys one life from the Jewish people ... anyone who saves one life from the Jewish people".
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Mishnah_Sanhedrin.4.5?version=english|Open%20Mishnah
- **Why:** Infinite worth of each person, and common descent as a check on pride. **Textual note:** printed editions read "a single soul _from Israel_"; the Kaufmann manuscript, the most important Mishnah witness, lacks it. I verified this in Sefaria's Kaufmann-based Hebrew edition (Dan Be'eri), which reads "שכל המאבד נפש אחת" with no "מישראל"; the standard printed text (Torat Emet) reads "נפש אחת מישראל". Scholars also report the shorter reading in the Yerushalmi, but I did not check that myself. Present it honestly. Qur'an 5:32 explicitly draws on this tradition.

### 12. Babylonian Talmud, Sotah 14a (imitating God's attributes)

> Rather, To follow the character traits of G-d. "Just as he clothes the naked, as it is written, 'And the Lord G-d made for Adam and his wife cloaks of leather, and he clothed them (Genesis 3:21);' so too you shall clothe the naked. The Holy One, Blessed be He, visited the sick, as it is written, 'And he appeared in Ailonei Mamrei [while Abraam was in pain] (Genesis 18:1);' so too you shall visit the sick. The Holy One, Blessed be He, comforted mourners, as it is written, 'And it was, after the death of Abraham, and G-d blessed his son Isaac (Genesis 25:11);' so too you shall comfort mourners. The Holy One, Blessed be He, buried the dead, as it is written, 'And he buried him in the valley (Deuteronomy 34:6);' so too, you shall bury the dead."

- **Translation:** Sefaria Community Translation (**CC0**)
- **Source URL:** https://www.sefaria.org/api/v3/texts/Sotah.14a?version=english|Sefaria%20Community%20Translation (segment 4)
- **Why:** Imitatio Dei as concrete deeds of kindness (gemilut chasadim). It parallels Matthew 25:35-40 strikingly. Long; could be trimmed to the first and last "so too" lines.

**Also verified (Taylor, same chapter URLs):** Avot 1:18, "Rabban Shime'on ben Gamliel said, On three things the world stands; on Judgment, and on Truth, and on Peace." Avot 4:3, "He used to say, Despise not any man, and carp not at any thing; for thou wilt find that there is not a man that has not his hour, and not a thing that has not its place." Avot 4:21, "R. Li'ezer ha-Qappar said, Jealousy, and lust, and ambition, put a man out of the world."

## Beware

- **"Do not be daunted by the enormity of the world's grief. Do justly, now. Love mercy, now. Walk humbly, now..."** is widely posted as "the Talmud". It is a modern interpretive paraphrase (associated with Rami Shapiro's renderings of Avot), loosely based on Tarfon in Avot 2:16 and Micah 6:8. It is not a translation.
- **"We do not see things as they are, we see them as we are"** is often labelled "Talmud". It is not found there; it is usually traced to Anaïs Nin (who herself attributed it to "the Talmud", without a source).
- **"Whoever saves one life saves the world entire"** is the _Schindler's List_ wording, which drops the Mishnah's textual variant (see #11). Cite the Mishnah, not the film.
- Avot sayings are attributed by tradition. Historians treat attribution to individual sages as uncertain, especially for the earliest (Simeon the Just, the Men of the Great Assembly).
- Shabbat 31a's Hillel formula is _negative_ ("what is hateful... do not do"). Don't silently convert it to the positive Matthew 7:12 form.

## Bridges

1. **Golden rule.** Hillel's negative form (Shabbat 31a) vs **Christianity**, Matthew 7:12 / Luke 6:31 (positive form), and the **Didache 1:2** ("all things whatsoever thou wouldst should not occur to thee, thou also to another do not do"), which keeps Hillel's negative form inside early Christianity. Also **Islam**, Nawawi 13 ("loves for his brother what he loves for himself"), and **Confucianism**, _Analects_ 15:24.
2. **Doing good for its own sake / in secret.** Antigonus (Avot 1:3) serving "without a view to receive recompense" pairs with **Christianity**, Matthew 6:3-4 (alms in secret), **Islam**, Qur'an 2:264 (charity "to be seen of men" is void), and **Stoicism** (virtue as its own reward). It is in tension with John 3:21's "comes to the light, that his works may be revealed", which is about integrity having nothing to hide rather than display.
3. **One life, a whole world.** Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5 is consciously echoed in **Islam, Qur'an 5:32** ("We decreed for the Children of Israel that whosoever killeth a human being ... it shall be as if he had killed all mankind"). This is a rare explicit textual bridge between two traditions.
4. **Doing over saying.** Avot 1:17 ("not learning but doing") meets **Christianity**, James 1:22 and 1 John 3:18 ("not ... in word only ... but in deed and truth"), and **Stoicism**, Epictetus's insistence on practice over lecture.
5. **Self-mastery as true strength.** Ben Zoma (Avot 4:1, quoting Prov 16:32) meets the **Islamic** hadith "He is not strong ... who throws people down, but he is the strong among us who masters himself when angry", and **Stoicism**, Marcus Aurelius's rule over the "ruling faculty" (hegemonikon).
6. **Death and unfinished work.** Tarfon (Avot 2:16) and Hillel's "perchance thou mayest not have leisure" (2:4) meet **Hebrew Bible**, Ecclesiastes 9:10, and **Stoicism**, Seneca's _On the Shortness of Life_.
