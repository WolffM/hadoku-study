# Jainism

## Overview

Jainism (_Jaina dharma_, जैन धर्म) is the path taught by the _Jinas_ (जिन,
"conquerors"), also called _Tīrthaṅkaras_ (तीर्थङ्कर, "ford-makers"), who
conquered attachment and aversion in themselves and then showed others the
crossing out of the cycle of rebirth (_saṃsāra_, संसार). Its ethical centre is
_ahiṃsā_ (अहिंसा), non-injury to every living being (_jīva_, जीव), down to the
smallest creatures. The five great vows (_mahāvrata_, महाव्रत) are non-injury,
truthfulness (_satya_), not stealing (_asteya_), chastity (_brahmacarya_) and
non-possession (_aparigraha_, अपरिग्रह). Householders keep lighter versions
(_aṇuvrata_). The intellectual counterpart of non-violence is _anekāntavāda_
(अनेकान्तवाद), "many-sidedness": no single human viewpoint grasps the whole
truth, so humility in argument is itself a form of non-harm.

The path to liberation (_mokṣa_) is summed up as the "three jewels"
(_ratnatraya_): right faith/insight, right knowledge, right conduct
(Tattvārtha Sūtra 1.1). Karma is understood almost physically, as fine matter
that clings to the soul through passions; the soul's native state is infinite
knowledge, perception and bliss, a radiant purity obscured by karmic
"coverings". This makes Jain imagery of light rather striking: the Jina is
"the luminary, who brings light into the whole world of living beings"
(Uttarādhyayana 23.78).

Historical setting: the 24th Tīrthaṅkara, Vardhamāna Mahāvīra, was an older
contemporary of the Buddha in the Ganges valley (traditionally 599-527 BCE;
many scholars prefer c. 5th century BCE). His predecessor Pārśva is widely
accepted as historical. The canonical texts (_Āgamas_) are in Ardhamāgadhī
Prakrit; the Śvetāmbara canon was fixed at the council of Valabhī (5th century
CE). The Ācārāṅga Sūtra, Book 1, is considered among the oldest layers. The
Digambara sect does not accept these Āgamas as authentic, but both sects
accept Umāsvāti's Sanskrit _Tattvārtha Sūtra_ (c. 2nd-5th century CE).
Jainism is a living tradition of some 4-5 million people, mostly in India and
the diaspora.

## Candidate passages

### 1. Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.4.1.1 (the "pure, unchangeable, eternal law")

> The Arhats and Bhagavats of the past, present, and future, all say thus, speak thus, declare thus, explain thus: all breathing, existing, living, sentient creatures should not be slain, nor treated with violence, nor abused, nor tormented, nor driven away. (1)
>
> This is the pure, unchangeable, eternal law, which the clever ones, who understand the world, have declared …

- Translation: Jacobi, SBE 22 (1884). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/acaranga-sutra/d/doc424417.html (Book 1, Lecture 4, Lesson 1)
- Why: The foundational statement of _ahiṃsā_: the four-fold list of living things and five-fold list of harms. The first sentence stands alone well.

### 2. Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.2.3.4

> For there is nothing inaccessible for death. All beings are fond of life, like pleasure, hate pain, shun destruction, like life, long to live. To all life is dear. (4)

- Translation: Jacobi (1884). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/acaranga-sutra/d/doc424409.html (Book 1, Lecture 2, Lesson 3)
- Why: Death and non-violence in three sentences; the reason not to harm is that every being shares our wish to live.

### 3. Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.5.5.4 (the Jain golden rule)

> As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to kill. As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to tyrannise over. As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to torment.

- Translation: Jacobi (1884). PD. (The next sentence on wisdomlib reads "I n the same way…", a transcription typo; it is omitted here.)
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/acaranga-sutra/d/doc424425.html (Book 1, Lecture 5, Lesson 5)
- Why: Golden rule stated as identification with the victim. Strong bridge passage.

### 4. Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.3.1.1

> The unwise sleep, the sages always wake. Know, that in this world the (cause of) misery brings forth evil consequences! Knowing the course of the world, one should cease from violent acts.

- Translation: Jacobi (1884). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/acaranga-sutra/d/doc424413.html (Book 1, Lecture 3, Lesson 1)
- Why: Sleep/waking as ignorance/insight (compare Romans 13:11-12, "the night is far spent"). The first sentence alone is a fine display line.

### 5. Ācārāṅga Sūtra I.3.3.4

> Man! Thou art thy own friend; why wishest thou for a friend beyond thyself? … Man, understand well the truth! exerting himself in the rule of truth a wise man overcomes Māra.

- Translation: Jacobi (1884). PD. Ellipsis omits an obscure sentence about "a dweller on high".
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/acaranga-sutra/d/doc424415.html (Book 1, Lecture 3, Lesson 3)
- Why: Self-reliance plus "the rule of truth" overcoming Māra (death/temptation). Pairs with Gītā 6.5 and Dhammapada 160.

### 6. Sūtrakṛtāṅga I.1.4.10 (repeated at I.11.10)

> This is the quintessence of wisdom: not to kill anything. Know this to be the legitimate conclusion from the principle of the reciprocity with regard to non-killing. (10)

- Translation: Jacobi, SBE 45 (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/sutrakritanga/d/doc424284.html (Book 1, Lecture 1, Chapter 4); same verse at https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/sutrakritanga/d/doc424301.html (Lecture 11, "The Path")
- Why: Short, quotable, and names "reciprocity" (Jacobi's word for _samatā_, equality) as the basis of non-violence.

### 7. Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 9.34-36 (King Nami)

> ‘Though a man should conquer thousands and thousands of valiant (foes), greater will be his victory if he conquers nobody but himself. (34)
>
> ‘Fight with your Self; why fight with external foes? He who conquers himself through himself, will obtain happiness. (35)
>
> 'The five senses, anger, pride, delusion, and greed
> —difficult to conquer is one’s self; but when that is conquered, everything is conquered.' (36)

- Translation: Jacobi, SBE 45 (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/uttaradhyayana-sutra/d/doc424219.html (Chapter 9, "The Pravragya of King Nami")
- Why: The meaning of _Jina_, "conqueror", in one stanza. Nearly identical to Dhammapada 103 and Proverbs 16:32.

### 8. Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 10.1-2 ("The Leaf of the Tree")

> As the fallow leaf of the tree falls to the ground, when its days are gone, even so the life of men (will come to its close); Gautama, be careful all the while! (1)
>
> As a dew-drop dangling on the top of a blade of Kuśa-grass lasts but a short time, even so the life of men; Gautama, be careful all the while! (2)

- Translation: Jacobi (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/uttaradhyayana-sutra/d/doc424220.html (Chapter 10)
- Why: Mahāvīra to his disciple Gautama on mortality; the refrain ("be careful", _apramāda_, vigilance) recurs through the whole chapter. Compare Psalm 103:15-16 and James 4:14.

### 9. Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 23.75-78 (Keśi and Gautama)

> ‘In this dreadfully dark gloom there live many beings; who will bring light into the whole world of living beings?’ (75)
>
> “The spotless sun has risen which illuminates the whole world; he will bring light into the whole world of living beings.” (76)
>
> Keśi said to Gautama, ‘What do you call this sun?’ To these words of Keśi Gautama made the following reply: (77)
>
> “Risen has he who put an end to the Circle of Births, the omniscient Jina, the luminary, who brings light into the whole world of living beings.” (78)

- Translation: Jacobi (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/uttaradhyayana-sutra/d/doc424233.html (Chapter 23)
- Why: The strongest Jain light/darkness passage and a close echo of John 1:4-9 and 3:19 ("light is come into the world"). Verses 75-76 alone also work.

### 10. Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 25.31-33 ("The True Sacrifice")

> "One does not become a Śramaṇa by the tonsure, nor a Brāhmaṇa by the sacred syllable oṃ, nor a Muni by living in the woods, nor a Tāpasa by wearing (clothes of) Kuśa-grass and bark. (31)
>
> "One becomes a Śramaṇa by equanimity, a Brāhmaṇa by chastity, a Muni by knowledge, and a Tāpasa by penance. (32)
>
> "By one’s actions one becomes a Brāhmaṇa, or a Kṣattriya, or a Vaiśya, or a Śūdra. (33)

- Translation: Jacobi (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/uttaradhyayana-sutra/d/doc424235.html (Chapter 25)
- Why: Inner conduct over outward marks and birth. Parallels Dhammapada ch. 26 and Jesus on whited sepulchres (Matthew 23:27). Same chapter, v. 24: "He who does not speak untruth from anger or for fun, from greed or from fear, him we call a Brāhmaṇa."

### 11. Uttarādhyayana Sūtra 1.15

> Subdue your Self, for the Self is difficult to subdue; if your Self is subdued, you will be happy in this world and in the next. (15)

- Translation: Jacobi (1895). PD.
- Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/uttaradhyayana-sutra/d/doc424211.html (Chapter 1, "On Discipline")
- Why: One-sentence version of passage 7; good if a shorter text is wanted.

### 12. Tattvārtha Sūtra 1.1 (the Three Jewels)

> Right faith (or perception), right knowledge, and right
> conduct constitute the way to moksha.

- Translation: J. L. Jaini, _Outlines of Jainism_ (Cambridge UP, 1916), "XV. The Three Jewels", no. 65. PD.
- Source: https://archive.org/download/outlinesofjainis00jain/outlinesofjainis00jain_djvu.txt
- Sanskrit (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/तत्त्वार्थसूत्रम्/प्रथमोऽध्यायः): सम्यग्दर्शन ज्ञान चारित्राणि मोक्षमार्गः।।
- Why: The opening sūtra of the one text all Jains share. Faith, knowledge and conduct together, in the spirit of "he that doeth truth".

### Reserve (verified)

- **Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.21**, Sanskrit only (https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/तत्त्वार्थसूत्रम्/पञ्चमोऽध्यायः): परस्परोपग्रहो जीवानाम्।। (_parasparopagraho jīvānām_, "souls support one another"; this is the modern Jain motto). No PD English translation was fetched; the common English "Souls render service to one another" is Tatia's (1994, in copyright). A PD option would be to quote the Sanskrit with the owner's own literal gloss.
- **Ten virtues (_daśa-dharma_)**, J. L. ## Bridges

1. **Light and darkness / John 3:19-21.** Uttarādhyayana 23.75-78 ("In this dreadfully dark gloom there live many beings; who will bring light…?") is close to John 1:5-9 and 8:12 ("I am the light of the world"). The Jain answer differs: the "sun" is a human being who conquered himself, not a divine Word, and every soul can become one.
2. **Golden rule.** Ācārāṅga I.5.5 ("As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to kill") with Mahābhārata Anuśāsana CXIII, Analects 15.24 and Matthew 7:12. The Jain form is the most radical of these because it covers insects and plants.
3. **Self-conquest.** Uttarādhyayana 9.34 with Dhammapada 103 ("If one man conquer in battle a thousand times thousand men, and if another conquer himself, he is the greatest of conquerors", Max Müller) and Proverbs 16:32 ("he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city"). These are closely related Indian traditions, so the Dhammapada parallel is a shared Śramaṇa heritage, not coincidence.
4. **Non-violence.** Jain _ahiṃsā_ directly shaped Gandhi through the Jain layman Shrimad Rajchandra, and through Gandhi, Martin Luther King Jr. Tension with Bhagavad Gītā 2 (Krishna urging Arjuna to fight) and with Christian just-war thought.
5. **Humility in knowledge.** _Anekāntavāda_ (many-sidedness; the parable of the blind men and the elephant, which also appears in Buddhist Udāna 6.4) with 1 Corinthians 13:12 ("now we see through a glass, darkly") and Zhuangzi's "frog in the well".
6. **Death.** Uttarādhyayana 10 (the falling leaf) beside Psalm 103:15-16, James 4:14, and the Jain practice of _sallekhanā_ (voluntary fasting at the end of life), which is a point of real ethical tension with other traditions and was litigated in Indian courts in 2015.

## Beware (misattributions and mis-renderings)

- **"Live and let live"** is widely called "Mahāvīra's message". It is a modern English slogan, not a canonical text.
- **"Thou art he whom thou intendest to kill"** circulates as Ācārāṅga 1.5.5. It is a modern rendering of the same passage; Jacobi's PD wording is "As it would be unto thee, so it is with him whom thou intendest to kill." Do not mix the two.
- **"Souls render service to one another"** (TS 5.21) is Tatia's 1994 translation, in copyright.
- **"Mahavira quotes"** on image sites ("Every soul is in itself absolutely omniscient and blissful", "The greatest mistake of a soul is non-recognition of its real self", etc.) generally give no canonical citation. Treat them as unverified unless traced to an Āgama.
- **"Ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ"** is often called the Jain motto. The phrase is from the Mahābhārata (Hindu), though Jains use it widely.
- **Dates:** "599-527 BCE" for Mahāvīra is the traditional Śvetāmbara dating; many historians put him later. Present it as traditional.
