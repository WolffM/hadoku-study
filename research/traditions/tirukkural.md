# Tirukkural (திருக்குறள்) of Thiruvalluvar (திருவள்ளுவர்)

## Overview

The Tirukkural ("sacred couplets") is a Tamil treatise of 1,330 _kural_ (குறள்) couplets in 133 chapters (அதிகாரம் _athikaram_) of ten couplets each. It is divided into three books on the classical ends of life: அறம் _aram_ (virtue or righteousness, cognate in function with Sanskrit _dharma_), பொருள் _porul_ (wealth, polity and worldly affairs; _artha_), and இன்பம் _inbam_ (love; _kama_). Strikingly, it has no fourth book on liberation (_moksha_). The ethical vision is practical, universal and almost entirely non-sectarian. It praises அன்பு _anbu_ (love), வாய்மை _vaymai_ (truthfulness), கொல்லாமை _kollamai_ (not killing), இன்னா செய்யாமை _inna seyyamai_ (not doing harm), பொறை _porai_ (forbearance), ஈகை _eekai_ (giving) and விருந்தோம்பல் _virunthombal_ (hospitality). Virtue is located in the purity of the mind: "spotless be thou in mind" (kural 34).

The text belongs to the Tamil "Eighteen Minor Classics" (பதினெண்கீழ்க்கணக்கு _Pathinenkilkanakku_) of didactic literature. Its date is disputed. Traditional Tamil reckoning places it very early, and the Tamil Nadu government's Thiruvalluvar era begins in 31 BCE. Most modern scholars, notably Kamil Zvelebil, date it around 450-500 CE, with a range of 300 BCE-6th c. CE still argued. Of the author almost nothing is known. Later hagiographies (weaver of Mylapore, wife Vasuki) are legends. Because the text names no sectarian deity, Jains, Saivas, Vaishnavas, Buddhists and even Christian apologists have claimed it. Its strong emphasis on non-killing and vegetarianism (chs. 26 and 33) has led many scholars to see Jain influence, though this is not settled. In Tamil Nadu it functions as a civic scripture: it is called _Ulaga Podhumarai_ (உலகப் பொதுமறை), "the universal Veda", and couplets are displayed in government buses and offices.

The work has unusually rich resonance with the owner's first passage, John 3:19-21. Chapter 30, "Veracity" (வாய்மை), calls truth the only real lamp (299). It also says outward purity comes from water and inward purity from truthfulness (298), and that a lie knowingly told burns the liar's own heart (293).

## Candidate passages

Verse and prose are copied from the fetched English e-text. The Tamil is copied from the fetched Tamil e-text, exactly as given there.

### 1. Kural 34 (ch. 4, Assertion of the Strength of Virtue, அறன் வலியுறுத்தல்): A spotless mind

> Spotless be thou in mind! This only merits virtue's name;
> All else, mere pomp of idle sound, no real worth can claim.
>
> Let him who does virtuous deeds be of spotless mind; to that extent is virtue; all else is vain show.

- Tamil: மனத்துக்கண் மாசிலன் ஆதல் அனைத்து அறன் / ஆகுல நீர பிற.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0153.html; Tamil at https://www.projectmadurai.org/pm_etexts/utf8/pmuni0001.html
- Why: the Kural's most famous definition of virtue, as inner purity rather than display.

### 2. Kural 72 (ch. 8, The Possession of Love, அன்புடைமை): Love gives even its bones

> The loveless to themselves belong alone;
> The loving men are others' to the very bone.
>
> Those who are destitute of love appropriate all they have to themselves; but those who possess love cons ider [_sic_: "consider"] even their bones to belong to others.

- Tamil: அன்பிலார் எல்லாம் தமக்குரியர் அன்புடையார் / என்பும் உரியர் பிறர்க்கு. (The Project Madurai Tamil file mislabels this couplet "71". By position it is 72.)
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: a vivid, memorable picture of self-giving love.

### 3. Kural 151 (ch. 16, The Possession of Patience, Forbearance, பொறையுடைமை): The earth bears those who dig it

> As earth bears up the men who delve into her breast,
> To bear with scornful men of virtues is the best.
>
> To bear with those who revile us, just as the earth bears up those who dig it, is the first of virtues.

- Tamil: அகழ்வாரைத் தாங்கும் நிலம்போலத் தம்மை / இகழ்வார்ப் பொறுத்தல் தலை.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: a beautiful image of forbearance. Pairs with humility passages in other traditions.

### 4. Kural 293 (ch. 30, Veracity, வாய்மை): The lie that burns its teller

> Speak not a word which false thy own heart knows
> Self-kindled fire within the false one's spirit glows.
>
> Let not a man knowingly tell a lie; for after he has told the lie, his mind will burn him (with the memory of his guilt).

- Tamil: தன்நெஞ் சறிவது பொய்யற்க பொய்த்தபின் / தன்நெஞ்சே தன்னைச் சுடும்.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: conscience as an inner fire. A sharp, two-line ethic of truthfulness.

### 5. Kural 294 (ch. 30, Veracity): The truthful dwell in all hearts

> True to his inmost soul who lives,- enshrined
> He lives in souls of all mankind.
>
> He who, in his conduct, preserves a mind free from deceit, will dwell in the minds of all men.

- Tamil: உள்ளத்தாற் பொய்யா தொழுகின் உலகத்தார் / உள்ளத்து ளெல்லாம் உளன்.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: integrity is rewarded with communion rather than reputation.

### 6. Kural 298 (ch. 30, Veracity): Water cleans the body, truth the mind

> Outward purity the water will bestow;
> Inward purity from truth alone will flow.
>
> Purity of body is produced by water and purity of mind by truthfulness.

- Tamil: புறள்தூய்மை நீரான் அமையும் அகந்தூய்மை / வாய்மையால் காணப் படும். (As given in the Project Madurai file. Standard editions read புறந்தூய்மை. Check before display.)
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: **Strong resonance** with "he that doeth truth" (John 3:21) and with Jesus on cleaning the inside of the cup (Matthew 23:25-26).

### 7. Kural 299 (ch. 30, Veracity): The lamp of truth

> Every lamp is not a lamp in wise men's sight;
> That's the lamp with truth's pure radiance bright.
>
> All lamps of nature are not lamps; the lamp of truth is the lamp of the wise.

- Tamil: எல்லா விளக்கும் விளக்கல்ல சான்றோர்க்குப் / பொய்யா விளக்கே விளக்கு.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: **The best light/truth passage in the Kural**, and the most direct echo of John 3:19-21. Literally, the "un-lying lamp" (பொய்யா விளக்கு) is the only lamp for the noble.

### 8. Kural 314 (ch. 32, Not doing Evil, இன்னா செய்யாமை): Shame the wrongdoer with kindness

> To punish wrong, with kindly benefits the doers ply;
> Thus shame their souls; but pass the ill unheeded by.
>
> The (proper) punishment to those who have done evil (to you), is to put them to shame by showing them kindness, in return and to forget both the evil and the good done on both sides.

- Tamil: இன்னாசெய் தாரை ஒறுத்தல் அவர்நாண / நன்னயஞ் செய்து விடல்.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: returning good for evil. Probably the Kural's most quoted ethical couplet. Compare Romans 12:20 and Daodejing 63.

### 9. Kural 316 (ch. 32, Not doing Evil): The Kural's golden rule

> What his own soul has felt as bitter pain,
> From making others feel should man abstain.
>
> Let not a man consent to do those things to another which, he knows, will cause sorrow.

- Tamil: இன்னா எனத்தான் உணர்ந்தவை துன்னாமை / வேண்டும் பிறன்கண் செயல்.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: a clear negative golden rule grounded in one's own felt experience of pain.

### 10. Kural 321 (ch. 33, Not killing, கொல்லாமை): Not killing is the work of virtue

> What is the work of virtue? 'Not to kill';
> For 'killing' leads to every work of ill.
>
> Never to destroy life is the sum of all virtuous conduct. The destruction of life leads to every evil.

- Tamil: அறவினை யாதெனின் கொல்லாமை கோறல் / பிறவினை எல்லாந் தரும்.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: ahimsa stated as the summary of all virtue. A direct bridge to Jainism and Buddhism.

### 11. Kural 336 (ch. 34, Instability, நிலையாமை): Here yesterday, gone today

> Existing yesterday, today to nothing hurled!-
> Such greatness owns this transitory world.
>
> This world possesses the greatness that one who yesterday was is not today.

- Tamil: நெருநல் உளனொருவன் இன்றில்லை என்னும் / பெருமை உடைத்துஇவ் வுலகு.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: a death and impermanence couplet with dry irony: the world's "greatness" is that the living vanish.

### 12. Kural 423 (ch. 43, Wisdom, அறிவுடைமை): Discern the truth, whoever speaks it

> Though things diverse from divers sages' lips we learn,
> 'Tis wisdom's part in each the true thing to discern.
>
> To discern the truth in every thing, by whomsoever spoken, is wisdom.

- Tamil: எப்பொருள் யார்யார்வாய்க் கேட்பினும் அப்பொருள் / மெய்ப்பொருள் காண்ப தறிவு.
- Translation: Pope 1886 (verse); the prose rendering is from the same edition (Drew/Lazarus). Source: as above.
- Why: an ideal motto for a page that draws wisdom from all traditions.

## Bridges

1. **Truth as light: Kural 299 ↔ John 3:19-21 ↔ Matthew 5:14-16 (the lamp on a stand) ↔ Daodejing 52 ("Who uses well his light", Legge) ↔ Zhuangzi 23 (a settled heart "gives forth a divine radiance", Giles).** The Kural's "un-lying lamp" is truthfulness itself. In John, the one who does the truth comes to the light. Both join truth and light into one idea.
2. **Inner vs. outer purity: Kural 298 and 34 ↔ Matthew 23:25-26 and Mark 7:15 ↔ Dhammapada (ch. 1, mind as forerunner of all states) ↔ Shinto _makoto_ / _harae_.** The Kural deliberately demotes ritual washing in favour of truthfulness. This is a tension with purity-centred ritual traditions, including Brahmanical ones contemporary with it.
3. **Golden rule: Kural 316 ↔ Analects XV.23 (己所不欲，勿施於人; numbered XV.24 in some editions) ↔ Hillel (Shabbat 31a) ↔ Matthew 7:12 ↔ Mahabharata Anushasana Parva.** The Kural grounds the rule in remembered pain ("what he knows will cause sorrow"), which makes it experiential rather than reciprocal.
4. **Good for evil: Kural 314 and 987 ↔ Romans 12:20-21 (heaping coals by kindness) ↔ Daodejing 63 (報怨以德) ↔ tension with Analects XIV.36** ("recompense injury with justice"). The Kural's "put them to shame by kindness" is remarkably close to Paul.
5. **Non-violence: Kural 321 (and ch. 26, renouncing flesh) ↔ Jain _ahimsa_ (Acaranga Sutra I.4.1) ↔ the Buddhist first precept ↔ Dhammapada ch. 10 (all tremble at the rod).** This is the strongest evidence for the Jain-influence hypothesis.
6. **Death and impermanence: Kural 336 ↔ James 4:14 (life as a vapour) ↔ Psalm 103:15-16 ↔ Zhuangzi 32 (Zhuangzi's own death).**

## Beware (misattributions and pitfalls)

- **Viral "Thiruvalluvar quotes"** are often loose paraphrases of modern translations (Sundaram, Rajaji and others, which are copyrighted) or wholly invented motivational lines. Always cite the kural number and check it against the Tamil.
- **Pope's verse is a rhymed paraphrase.** It is sometimes padded for rhyme ("of virtues is the best"). For accuracy, prefer the prose rendering or show both, and attribute the prose to Drew/Lazarus via Pope's edition.
- **The St Thomas claim**: the story that Valluvar was taught by the Apostle Thomas, or that the Kural is Christian, is an unsupported 20th-century apologetic. Similarly, don't assert that it is definitively Jain, Saiva or Buddhist. Its affiliation is disputed.
- **Dating**: avoid "2,000 years old" as fact. Scholarly estimates range widely, and many now favour c. 5th c. CE.
- **Kural 1** ("Adi Bhagavan"): interpretations of the deity named are contested along sectarian lines. Present it neutrally.
- **Typos in the e-text**: the Project Madurai Tamil file has a few errors (couplet 72 is labelled "71"; 298 reads புறள்தூய்மை). Cross-check against a printed edition before display.
