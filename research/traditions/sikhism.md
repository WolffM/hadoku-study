# Sikhism

## Overview

Sikhism (Sikhī, ਸਿੱਖੀ) began with Guru Nanak (1469–1539) in the Punjab and was shaped by nine successor Gurus. Guru Arjan (fifth Guru) compiled the Ādi Granth in 1604; Guru Gobind Singh (tenth Guru, d. 1708) vested final Guruship in the scripture itself, now the **Guru Granth Sahib** (ਗੁਰੂ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਸਾਹਿਬ), 1,430 pages (_ang_, ਅੰਗ) of hymns (_gurbāṇī_, ਗੁਰਬਾਣੀ) by six of the Gurus and by Hindu and Muslim saint-poets (_bhagats_) such as Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas and Shaikh Farid. That inclusion is itself an ethical statement: holiness is not owned by one community. The scripture opens with the **Mūl Mantar** (ਮੂਲ ਮੰਤਰ), which names the One as true, creative, _nirbhau_ (ਨਿਰਭਉ, without fear) and _nirvair_ (ਨਿਰਵੈਰੁ, without enmity).

The ethical center is **truth** (_sach_, ਸਚੁ). The goal is to become _sachiār_ (ਸਚਿਆਰ), "truthful" or "true through and through", and the barrier is _haumai_ (ਹਉਮੈ, ego, literally "I-I"). The divine order (_hukam_, ਹੁਕਮ) is accepted rather than resisted. The divine light (_jot_, ਜੋਤਿ) is in every being, which grounds human equality and the rejection of caste distinction. Practice is summed up in a later formula: _nām japṇā_ (remembering the Name), _kirat karnī_ (honest work), and _vaṇḍ chhakṇā_ (sharing with others). Add _sevā_ (ਸੇਵਾ, selfless service), seen in the _langar_ free kitchen, and the daily prayer for _sarbat dā bhalā_ (ਸਰਬੱਤ ਦਾ ਭਲਾ), "the good of all". A widely quoted line holds that truth is higher than everything, _but higher still is truthful living_ (_sach āchār_, ਸਚੁ ਆਚਾਰੁ). Its verified wording is below (Ang 62).

For this site, Sikh scripture is unusually rich in **light/darkness** imagery that parallels John 3:19–21. Falsehood is a moonless night (Majh ki Var), the divine light shines in all things (Sohila), and the faces of those who have lived by the Name are "made bright" at the final reckoning (Japji's closing slok). The Japji, Guru Nanak's morning prayer, opens by asking how one becomes true and how "the veil of falsehood" is torn. Sikhism is a living faith with about 25–30 million adherents, and the Guru Granth Sahib is treated as a living Guru, not merely as a book. Gurmukhi text should be reproduced accurately and with its Ang reference.

## Candidate passages

### 1. Japji, Pauri 1: "How shall man become true?" (Ang 1)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Japji Sahib, pauri 1. Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 1.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 196.
- **Text (verbatim; OCR "Hitn" corrected to "Him", "Com¬ mander" rejoined):**
  > By thinking I cannot obtain a conception of Him, even though I think hundreds of thousands of times.
  > Even though I be silent and keep my attention firmly fixed on Him, I cannot preserve silence.
  > The hunger of the hungry for God subsideth not though they obtain the load of the worlds.
  > If man should have thousands and hundreds of thousands of devices, even one would not assist him in obtaining God.
  > How shall man become true before God? How shall the veil of falsehood be rent?
  > By walking, O Nanak, according to the will of the Commander as preordained.
- **Gurmukhi (last two lines):** ਕਿਵ ਸਚਿਆਰਾ ਹੋਈਐ ਕਿਵ ਕੂੜੈ ਤੁਟੈ ਪਾਲਿ ॥ ਹੁਕਮਿ ਰਜਾਈ ਚਲਣਾ ਨਾਨਕ ਲਿਖਿਆ ਨਾਲਿ ॥੧॥
- **URL:** https://archive.org/download/sikhreligionitsg01unse/sikhreligionitsg01unse_djvu.txt ; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/1/G
- **Why:** This is the foundational question of Sikh ethics, and the closest structural match to John 3:21. The aim is to _become_ true (_sachiārā_), and the obstacle is a "veil" (_pāl_, wall) of falsehood. The last two lines stand well on their own.

### 2. Mūl Mantar and its opening slok (Ang 1)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Mūl Mantar and the slok "Ād sach". Ang 1.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 195 (opening of "The Japji").
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > There is but one God whose name is true, the Creator, devoid of fear and enmity, immortal, unborn, self-existent; by the favour of the Guru.
  >
  > The True One was in the beginning; the True One was in the primal age.
  > The True One is now also, O Nanak; the True One also shall be.
- **Gurmukhi:** ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਕਰਤਾ ਪੁਰਖੁ ਨਿਰਭਉ ਨਿਰਵੈਰੁ ਅਕਾਲ ਮੂਰਤਿ ਅਜੂਨੀ ਸੈਭੰ ਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/1/G
- **Why:** This is the creed of Sikhism. _Nirbhau nirvair_ ("without fear, without enmity") is an ethical ideal as well as a description of God. Macauliffe's own footnote gives the alternative rendering "God was true in the beginning...", so add a note if it is displayed.

### 3. Japji, closing Slok: "Shall have their countenances made bright" (Ang 8)

- **Citation:** Japji Sahib, final slok (Macauliffe notes it is "generally believed to be the composition of Guru Angad"). Ang 8.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 217.
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > The air is the guru, water our father, and the great earth our mother;
  > Day and night are our two nurses, male and female, who set the whole world a-playing.
  > Merits and demerits shall be read out in the presence of the Judge.
  > According to men's acts, some shall be near, and others distant from God.
  > They who have pondered on the Name and departed after the completion of their toil,
  > Shall have their countenances made bright, O Nanak; how many shall be emancipated in company with them!
- **Gurmukhi:** ਪਵਣੁ ਗੁਰੂ ਪਾਣੀ ਪਿਤਾ ਮਾਤਾ ਧਰਤਿ ਮਹਤੁ ॥ … ਚੰਗਿਆਈਆ ਬੁਰਿਆਈਆ ਵਾਚੈ ਧਰਮੁ ਹਦੂਰਿ ॥ … ਨਾਨਕ ਤੇ ਮੁਖ ਉਜਲੇ ਕੇਤੀ ਛੁਟੀ ਨਾਲਿ ॥੧॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/8/G
- **Why:** This is the **strongest light-imagery match to John 3:19–21**. Deeds are "read out" in the open (compare "that his deeds may be made manifest"), and the faces of those who did the truth are made bright (_mukh ujle_, ਮੁਖ ਉਜਲੇ). It also has a lovely ecological opening and says the good are not saved alone.

### 4. Sohila (Ārtī), Rag Dhanasari: "The light which is in everything is Thine" (Ang 13)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Rag Dhanasari, in the bedtime prayer Sohila. Ang 13.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 259 ("The Sohila").
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > The light which is in everything is Thine, O Lord of light.
  > From its brilliancy everything is brilliant;
  > By the Guru's teaching the light becometh manifest.
  > What pleaseth Thee is the real worship.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਸਭ ਮਹਿ ਜੋਤਿ ਜੋਤਿ ਹੈ ਸੋਇ ॥ ਤਿਸ ਦੈ ਚਾਨਣਿ ਸਭ ਮਹਿ ਚਾਨਣੁ ਹੋਇ ॥ ਗੁਰ ਸਾਖੀ ਜੋਤਿ ਪਰਗਟੁ ਹੋਇ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/13/G
- **Why:** Four short lines that pair with John 1:9 ("the true Light, which lighteth every man") and with the Upanishadic "by His light all this shines". They come from Guru Nanak's cosmic _ārtī_, sung with the sky as the lamp-tray. Note: Macauliffe's vol. 1, pp. 82–83 has an earlier version of the same hymn that ends "...is the real arati".

### 5. Sri Rag Ashtapadi: "The practice of truth is superior to all else" (Ang 62)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Sri Rag, Ashtapadi, verses 5–6. Ang 62.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 273.
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > Everything is inferior to truth; the practice of truth is superior to all else.
  > Call every one exalted; let no one appear to thee low.
  > The one God fashioned the vessels, and it is His light that filleth the three worlds.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਸਚਹੁ ਓਰੈ ਸਭੁ ਕੋ ਉਪਰਿ ਸਚੁ ਆਚਾਰੁ ॥੫॥ ਸਭੁ ਕੋ ਊਚਾ ਆਖੀਐ ਨੀਚੁ ਨ ਦੀਸੈ ਕੋਇ ॥ ਇਕਨੈ ਭਾਂਡੇ ਸਾਜਿਐ ਇਕੁ ਚਾਨਣੁ ਤਿਹੁ ਲੋਇ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/62/G
- **Why:** This is the **verified source of the famous "Truth is high, but higher still is truthful living"**. That popular wording is a modern paraphrase, so display Macauliffe's instead. It ties truth-as-practice ("doeth truth", John 3:21) to equality and to light (_ik chānaṇ_, "one light").

### 6. Majh ki Var: "In this completely dark night of falsehood" (Ang 145)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Var Majh, slok (Macauliffe places it in Nanak's reply to the Sidhs on Mount Sumer). Ang 145.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 170 (Life of Guru Nanak). The same lines are also quoted in Macauliffe's Introduction (roman-numbered pages).
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > The Kal age is a knife, kings are butchers; justice hath taken wings and fled.
  > In this completely dark night of falsehood the moon of truth is never seen to rise.
  > I have become perplexed in my search:
  > In the darkness I find no way.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਕਲਿ ਕਾਤੀ ਰਾਜੇ ਕਾਸਾਈ ਧਰਮੁ ਪੰਖ ਕਰਿ ਉਡਰਿਆ ॥ ਕੂੜੁ ਅਮਾਵਸ ਸਚੁ ਚੰਦ੍ਰਮਾ ਦੀਸੈ ਨਾਹੀ ਕਹ ਚੜਿਆ ॥ ਹਉ ਭਾਲਿ ਵਿਕੁੰਨੀ ਹੋਈ ॥ ਆਧੇਰੈ ਰਾਹੁ ਨ ਕੋਈ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/145/G
- **Why:** This is the darkness half of John 3:19 ("men loved darkness rather than light"). Falsehood is the moonless night (_amāvas_) in which the moon of truth does not rise. It is also an unflinching piece of social criticism of the ruling powers.

### 7. Asa ki Var: "Man is known as true when truth is in his heart" (Ang 468)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Asa ki Var, slok to pauri 10 (Macauliffe's numbering). Ang 468.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, pp. 231–232.
- **Text (verbatim; excerpt, first two couplets and close):**
  > Man is known as true when truth is in his heart;
  > When the filth of falsehood departeth, man washeth his body clean.
  > Man is known as true when he beareth love to the True One;
  > When the mind is enraptured on hearing the Name, man attaineth the door of salvation.
  > [...]
  > Man shall be known as true when he receiveth true instruction;
  > Let man show mercy to living things and perform some works of charity.
  > [...]
  > Truth is the medicine for all; it removeth and washeth away sin.
- **Gurmukhi (first line):** ਸਚੁ ਤਾ ਪਰੁ ਜਾਣੀਐ ਜਾ ਰਿਦੈ ਸਚਾ ਹੋਇ ॥ (close: ਸਚੁ ਸਭਨਾ ਹੋਇ ਦਾਰੂ ਪਾਪ ਕਢੈ ਧੋਇ ॥)
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/468/G
- **Why:** This is a litany defining sincerity as inner truth that shows outwardly in love, mercy and charity. It is a direct parallel to "doing the truth". The first couplet works as a stand-alone quotation.

### 8. Asa ki War, Slok XIV: the simmal tree and lowliness (Ang 470)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Asa ki Var (Macauliffe: "Slok XIV"). Ang 470.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, pp. 236–237.
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > The simmal-tree of the desert is very tall and very thick.
  > Why should the birds which go to it with hopes depart disappointed?
  > Because its fruit is insipid, its flowers unwholesome, and its leaves useless.
  > The tree which yieldeth sweet fruit is lowly, O Nanak, but its qualities and virtues are exquisite.
  > Every one boweth to himself; no one boweth to another.
  > If anything be put into a scale and weighed, the side which descendeth is the heavier.
- **Gurmukhi (key line):** ਮਿਠਤੁ ਨੀਵੀ ਨਾਨਕਾ ਗੁਣ ਚੰਗਿਆਈਆ ਤਤੁ ॥ ("Sweetness and lowliness, Nanak, are the essence of virtues")
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/470/G
- **Why:** A self-contained **humility** parable with a memorable image (the heavier pan of the scale sinks). Note that Macauliffe renders _miṭhat nīvī_ loosely. The Gurmukhi literally says "sweetness and humility are the essence of virtue".

### 9. Asa ki War: "Why call her bad from whom are born kings?" (Ang 473)

- **Citation:** Guru Nanak, Asa ki Var, slok (Macauliffe: "Guru Nanak's remonstrance to a man who reviled the female sex"). Ang 473.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 244. Macauliffe renders _bhaṇḍ_ (ਭੰਡ, woman) as "vessel" and explains it in a footnote ("Woman is meant"). OCR "vesseH" corrected to "vessel".
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > In a vessel man is conceived, from a vessel he is born, with a vessel he is betrothed and married.
  > With a vessel he contracteth friendship; with a vessel he goeth through the world.
  > When one vessel dieth, another is sought for; to a vessel he is bound.
  > Why call her bad from whom are born kings?
  > From a vessel a vessel is born; none may exist without a vessel.
  > Nanak, only the one True God is independent of a vessel.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਭੰਡਿ ਜੰਮੀਐ ਭੰਡਿ ਨਿੰਮੀਐ ਭੰਡਿ ਮੰਗਣੁ ਵੀਆਹੁ ॥ … ਸੋ ਕਿਉ ਮੰਦਾ ਆਖੀਐ ਜਿਤੁ ਜੰਮਹਿ ਰਾਜਾਨ ॥ … ਨਾਨਕ ਭੰਡੈ ਬਾਹਰਾ ਏਕੋ ਸਚਾ ਸੋਇ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/473/G
- **Why:** A sixteenth-century defense of women's dignity, much cited by Sikhs. If displayed, add a gloss that "vessel" = woman (_bhaṇḍ_), or show only the key line "Why call her bad from whom are born kings?"

### 10. Shaikh Farid, Slok 78: "Farid, do good for evil" (Ang 1381–1382)

- **Citation:** Shaikh Farid (Sufi, c. 1173–1266; bhagat in the Guru Granth Sahib), Salok 78. Ang 1381–1382.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 6, p. 406 ("LXXVIII"). The same couplet is quoted in vol. 3 (Life of Guru Arjan), on Guru Arjan's lips.
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > Farid, do good for evil, clothe not thy heart with anger:
  > Thus shall thy body not suffer pain, and thou shalt obtain everything.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਫਰੀਦਾ ਬੁਰੇ ਦਾ ਭਲਾ ਕਰਿ ਗੁਸਾ ਮਨਿ ਨ ਹਢਾਇ ॥ ਦੇਹੀ ਰੋਗੁ ਨ ਲਗਈ ਪਲੈ ਸਭੁ ਕਿਛੁ ਪਾਇ ॥੭੮॥
- **URL:** https://archive.org/download/sikhreligionitsg06unse/sikhreligionitsg06unse_djvu.txt ; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/1381/G , https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/1382/G
- **Why:** The most quotable **non-retaliation** couplet in the scripture. A Muslim Sufi's words are canonized in Sikh scripture, which is itself a bridge between traditions. Attribute it to Farid, not to a Guru.

### 11. Kabir, Prabhati 1: "The light hath dawned, the darkness is dispelled" (Ang 1349)

- **Citation:** Bhagat Kabir, Rag Bibhas Prabhati, hymn 1. Ang 1349.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 6, p. 276. OCR "confeneth" is presumably "conferreth" (I have not verified this against the print, so show it as given or drop the line).
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > My dread of transmigration is at an end
  > Since God displayed His love for me.
  > The light hath dawned, the darkness is dispelled;
  > I have obtained the jewel God by meditation on Him.
- **Gurmukhi (key line):** ਪ੍ਰਗਟੀ ਜੋਤਿ ਮਿਟਿਆ ਅੰਧਿਆਰਾ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/1349/G
- **Why:** A dawn hymn (_prabhātī_ = morning). Light comes and darkness goes, and the fear of death and rebirth ends. It pairs with John 3:19 and 1 John 2:8 ("the darkness is past, and the true light now shineth").

### 12. Japji, Pauri 20: washing the mind (Ang 4)

- **Citation:** Japji Sahib, pauri 20. Ang 4.
- **Translation:** Macauliffe 1909, vol. 1, p. 205.
- **Text (verbatim):**
  > When the hands, feet, and other members of the body are covered with filth,
  > It is removed by washing with water.
  > When thy clothes are polluted,
  > Apply soap, and the impurity shall be washed away.
  > So when the mind is defiled by sin,
  > It is cleansed by the love of the Name.
  > Men do not become saints or sinners by merely calling themselves so.
- **Gurmukhi:** ਭਰੀਐ ਹਥੁ ਪੈਰੁ ਤਨੁ ਦੇਹ ॥ ਪਾਣੀ ਧੋਤੈ ਉਤਰਸੁ ਖੇਹ ॥ … ਭਰੀਐ ਮਤਿ ਪਾਪਾ ਕੈ ਸੰਗਿ ॥ ਓਹੁ ਧੋਪੈ ਨਾਵੈ ਕੈ ਰੰਗਿ ॥ ਪੁੰਨੀ ਪਾਪੀ ਆਖਣੁ ਨਾਹਿ ॥
- **URL:** as above; https://api.banidb.com/v2/angs/4/G
- **Why:** A homely, concrete image of inner purification. The last line ("not by calling themselves so") is a sharp anti-hypocrisy maxim. (The original's punctuation after "filth." and "polluted." is normalized to commas here. Show the original periods if you want strict fidelity.)

## Bridges

1. **John 3:19–21 (Christianity).** Three passages bracket the whole Johannine passage. Majh ki Var's "dark night of falsehood" (#6) matches "men loved darkness". Japji 1's "How shall man become true...?" (#1) and Sri Rag's "the practice of truth is superior to all else" (#5) match "he that doeth truth". Japji's closing slok (#3), where deeds are read out and faces made bright, matches "cometh to the light, that his deeds may be made manifest". _Sach āchār_ is close to a Punjabi rendering of "doing the truth".
2. **Upanishads (Hindu Dharma), Katha Upanishad 2.2.15 / Mundaka 2.2.10.** "Everything shines after him who shines; by his light all this is lighted". Compare Sohila (#4): "From its brilliancy everything is brilliant." The Gurus spoke within Indic vocabulary (_jot_) while rejecting caste and ritualism. _(Check the exact wording against the vedanta file's Max Müller text.)_
3. **Golden rule / non-retaliation.** Farid's "do good for evil" (#10) parallels Romans 12:21 ("overcome evil with good"), Dhammapada 5 ("hatred does not cease by hatred"), and Tirukkural 314 (punish wrongdoers by doing them good until they are ashamed). Shotoku's Article X (bushido file) adds "dread our own faults" when others rage.
4. **Humility.** The simmal tree (#8), where the heavier pan sinks, parallels Daodejing 66 and 78 (rivers rule the valleys by being lower; water is weakest yet overcomes the hard), Luke 14:11 ("he that humbleth himself shall be exalted"), and Shinto oracle "even a hair of pride shuts you off from the Gods" (bushido file).
5. **Tension: dignity of women.** Asa ki Var's defense of women (#9) stands in productive tension with Manusmriti 9.3 ("a woman is not fit for independence"). Many Hindu reformers also contested that text. The pairing shows that every tradition holds internal debates.
6. **Fearlessness and death.** _Nirbhau nirvair_ (#2) and Kabir's "dread of transmigration is at an end" (#11) parallel 1 John 4:18 ("perfect love casteth out fear") and the Hagakure's demand to be free of fear of death. The contrast is instructive: for the Gurus fearlessness comes from love of God, not from a warrior's resolve.

## Further reading

- W. H. McLeod, _Guru Nanak and the Sikh Religion_ (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1968). A foundational critical study. Some of McLeod's historical conclusions were controversial among Sikh scholars, so read it alongside Sikh responses.
- Eleanor Nesbitt, _Sikhism: A Very Short Introduction_ (Oxford University Press, 2005; 2nd ed. 2016).
- Nikky-Guninder Kaur Singh, _Sikhism: An Introduction_ (I.B. Tauris, 2011). Also her _The Feminine Principle in the Sikh Vision of the Transcendent_ (Cambridge University Press, 1993).
- Pashaura Singh & Louis E. Fenech (eds.), _The Oxford Handbook of Sikh Studies_ (Oxford University Press, 2014).
- Arvind-Pal Singh Mandair, _Sikhism: A Guide for the Perplexed_ (Bloomsbury, 2013).

## Beware

- **"Truth is high, but higher still is truthful living."** The idea is genuine (Ang 62, #5 above), but this wording is a modern paraphrase, not a translation. Quote a real translation with the Ang reference.
- **"Recognize the whole human race as one" (ਮਾਨਸ ਕੀ ਜਾਤ ਸਬੈ ਏਕੈ ਪਹਿਚਾਨਬੋ).** This is by Guru Gobind Singh in the _Akal Ustat_ of the **Dasam Granth**, not in the Guru Granth Sahib and not by Guru Nanak. It is often misattributed.
- **Bhagats are not Gurus.** Kabir, Farid, Namdev and Ravidas are in the scripture but are not Sikh Gurus. Attribute to "Shaikh Farid, in the Guru Granth Sahib", not to "Guru Nanak".
- **Image-macro "Guru Nanak quotes"** often have no Ang reference, and many are inspirational paraphrases. Check any candidate against BaniDB before using it.
- **Do not copy the Sant Singh Khalsa translation.** It is everywhere online but in copyright.
- **Avoid the Trumpp (1877) translation**, even though it is public domain (see the table).
