# Hebrew Bible (Tanakh: Torah, Prophets, Writings)

## Overview

The Hebrew Bible (Tanakh, an acronym of **Torah** "instruction/teaching", **Nevi'im** "Prophets", **Ketuvim** "Writings") is a library put together over roughly a millennium (c. 10th-2nd centuries BCE) by the people of ancient Israel and Judah. Its ethics rest on a covenant (**berit**) between God and a people. Right conduct is answering a God who acts in history, and law, story, prophecy and proverb all shape what that answer looks like. Key terms: **tsedeq / tsedaqah** (righteousness, justice that restores right relationships; later also "charity"), **mishpat** (justice, right judgment), **chesed** (steadfast love, loyal kindness), **emet** (truth, faithfulness), **shalom** (wholeness, peace), and **yir'at YHWH** ("the fear of the LORD", reverent awe, called "the beginning of wisdom").

The Torah grounds ethics in memory: "you were strangers in the land of Egypt" is the reason given for protecting the foreigner, the widow and the orphan. The eighth-century prophets (Amos, Hosea, Isaiah, Micah) attack worship that goes on alongside injustice. Their famous line is that God wants justice and kindness rather than sacrifice. The wisdom books (**chokhmah** literature: Proverbs, Job, Ecclesiastes/Qohelet) are more international and empirical in tone. They share forms and even material with Egyptian and Mesopotamian instruction literature. Proverbs 22:17-24:22, for example, closely parallels the Egyptian _Instruction of Amenemope_. They also explore the limits of wisdom, as in Job and Qohelet.

Light and darkness run through the whole corpus: the righteous path is "like the dawning light", the wicked "stumble" in darkness, and a person's spirit is "the lamp of the LORD" that searches the inner self. Death is mostly faced without a developed afterlife (Sheol is a shadowy realm of the dead). That gives Ecclesiastes its urgent "whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might."

### 1. Micah 6:8

> He has shown you, O man, what is good. What does Yahweh require of you, but to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God?

- **Translation:** World English Bible (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/MIC06.htm
- **Why:** It may be the most compact statement of prophetic ethics anywhere: justice, kindness (chesed) and humility in one sentence.

### 2. Amos 5:24

> But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/AMO05.htm
- **Why:** One line, vivid image; the climax of Amos's attack on worship without justice (5:21-23). Famously quoted by Martin Luther King Jr.

### 3. Proverbs 4:18-19

> But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light that shines more and more until the perfect day. The way of the wicked is like darkness. They don't know what they stumble over.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/PRO04.htm
- **Why:** A direct Hebrew-wisdom antecedent to John 3:19-21's light/darkness ethics; self-contained.

### 4. Proverbs 20:27

> The spirit of man is Yahweh's lamp, searching all his innermost parts.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/PRO20.htm
- **Why:** Conscience as an inner light that exposes. A striking one-liner that pairs well with "doing truth comes to the light".

### 5. Isaiah 58:10

> and if you pour out your soul to the hungry, and satisfy the afflicted soul, then your light will rise in darkness, and your obscurity will be as the noonday;

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/ISA58.htm
- **Why:** Light earned by deeds of mercy, not by ritual. The verse begins mid-sentence ("and if..."), so it might need to be shown with 58:7 (verified on the same page: "Isn't it to distribute your bread to the hungry, and that you bring the poor who are cast out to your house? When you see the naked, that you cover him; and that you not hide yourself from your own flesh?").

### 6. Leviticus 19:18

> "'You shall not take vengeance, nor bear any grudge against the children of your people; but you shall love your neighbor as yourself. I am Yahweh.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/LEV19.htm
- **Why:** The source of "love your neighbor as yourself", cited by Jesus (Mark 12:31) and by Rabbi Akiva as "a great principle of the Torah". The opening quote marks belong to the nested speech; drop them for display.

### 7. Leviticus 19:34

> The stranger who lives as a foreigner with you shall be to you as the native-born among you, and you shall love him as yourself; for you lived as foreigners in the land of Egypt. I am Yahweh your God.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/LEV19.htm
- **Why:** Extends neighbor-love to the outsider and grounds it in memory of one's own suffering. Less familiar than 19:18 and arguably stronger.

### 8. Proverbs 25:21-22

> If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat. If he is thirsty, give him water to drink; for you will heap coals of fire on his head, and Yahweh will reward you.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/PRO25.htm
- **Why:** Kindness to enemies in the Hebrew Bible itself. Paul quotes it in Romans 12:20, a ready-made bridge.

### 9. Proverbs 3:27

> Don't withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in the power of your hand to do it.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/PRO03.htm
- **Why:** Plain, practical and universal; reads like a Stoic or Confucian maxim.

### 10. Deuteronomy 30:19

> I call heaven and earth to witness against you today that I have set before you life and death, the blessing and the curse. Therefore choose life, that you may live, you and your descendants,

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/DEU30.htm
- **Why:** The "two ways" choice that the Didache, the Dead Sea Scrolls and Zoroastrian thought all echo. It ends with a comma (the sentence continues into 30:20), so display it with "..." or stop at "choose life".

### 11. Ecclesiastes 9:10

> Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might; for there is no work, nor plan, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in Sheol, where you are going.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/ECC09.htm
- **Why:** Facing death squarely makes present action urgent. Pairs with Stoic and Epicurean reflections on death.

### 12. Ecclesiastes 7:2

> It is better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting; for that is the end of all men, and the living should take this to heart.

- **Translation:** WEB (PD)
- **Source URL:** https://ebible.org/eng-web/ECC07.htm
- **Why:** A biblical memento mori; countercultural and quotable.

**Also verified in the same files (backups):** Isaiah 5:20 ("Woe to those who call evil good, and good evil; who put darkness for light, and light for darkness; who put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!", ISA05.htm); Proverbs 15:1 ("A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.", PRO15.htm); Proverbs 16:18 ("Pride goes before destruction, and an arrogant spirit before a fall.", PRO16.htm).

## Beware

- **"Pride goeth before a fall"**: a folk compression of Prov 16:18, which actually says "Pride goes before destruction, and an arrogant spirit before a fall."
- **"Spare the rod, spoil the child"**: not biblical wording. It comes from Samuel Butler's _Hudibras_ (1663), loosely after Prov 13:24.
- **"God helps those who help themselves"**: not in the Bible (Algernon Sidney / Benjamin Franklin's _Poor Richard_).
- **"Money is the root of all evil"**: a misquote of 1 Timothy 6:10 ("the love of money"), and New Testament anyway.
- **"This too shall pass"**: not biblical; it comes from a Persian/Sufi-attributed fable.
- **"Cleanliness is next to godliness"**: John Wesley's sermon, not scripture.
- Watch for Ecclesiastes being quoted as if Qohelet's skeptical voice were the book's final word. 12:13 ("Fear God and keep his commandments") is an epilogue many scholars attribute to a later editor.

## Bridges

1. **Light/darkness and doing truth.** Proverbs 4:18-19 (the dawning path vs the stumbling dark) and Proverbs 20:27 (the spirit as the LORD's lamp searching the inner parts) are the Hebrew wisdom background to **Christianity, John 3:19-21** (already chosen). They also fit **Zoroastrianism's** Gathas, Yasna 30, with their primal choice between the truthful (asha) and the deceitful (druj) spirit.
2. **Two ways / choose life.** Deuteronomy 30:19 shows up directly in the **Didache 1:1** ("There are two ways, one of life and one of death") and in the Zoroastrian moral dualism of choice. Contrast **Stoicism** (Epictetus, _Enchiridion_ 1), where the choice is about what is "up to us" rather than covenant obedience.
3. **Golden rule.** Leviticus 19:18 is the base text for **Rabbinic Judaism's** Hillel, Shabbat 31a ("What is hateful to you, do not do to your neighbor"), for **Christianity's** Matthew 7:12, and for the **Islamic** hadith "none of you believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself" (Nawawi 13). Leviticus 19:34 pushes it to the foreigner, a pointed parallel to **Qur'an 49:13** ("nations and tribes that ye may know one another").
4. **Kindness to enemies.** Proverbs 25:21-22 is quoted by **Paul, Romans 12:20-21**, and resonates with **Qur'an 41:34** ("Repel the evil deed with one which is better").
5. **Death and present action.** Ecclesiastes 9:10 and 7:2 meet **Stoicism** (Marcus Aurelius's repeated reflections on mortality) and **Epicureanism** (Letter to Menoeceus, "death is nothing to us"). The tension: Qohelet finds death troubling but clarifying, while Epicurus argues it should not trouble us at all. Ecclesiastes 9:7-9 is also a well-known parallel to the alewife **Siduri's advice in the Old Babylonian Gilgamesh (Mesopotamian)**.
6. **Humility.** Micah 6:8's "walk humbly" pairs with **Qur'an 25:63** ("walk upon the earth modestly") and with Daoist and Confucian valuations of lowliness.
