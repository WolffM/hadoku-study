# Chan / Zen

## Overview

Chan 禪 (Japanese Zen, Korean Seon 선, Vietnamese Thiền; from Sanskrit _dhyāna_ ध्यान, "meditation") is the Mahāyāna Buddhist lineage that took shape in Tang-dynasty China (7th–9th c.). It claims to transmit awakening "directly pointing at the human mind; seeing one's nature, becoming Buddha" (直指人心，見性成佛). Its ethical vision is not a separate code layered on top of meditation. It holds that a mind which sees its own original nature (自性 _zìxìng_, Buddha-nature 佛性) naturally acts without crookedness.

The Platform Sutra puts it this way: "straightforward mind is the place of practice" (直心是道場). The text warns against speaking straight with the mouth while the mind is crooked (莫心行諂曲，口但說直). That is a direct counterpart to "doing the truth" in John 3:21. Other key terms:

- **no-thought**: 無念 _wúniàn_
- **non-duality**: 不二 _bù'èr_
- **picking and choosing**: 揀擇, which the _Xinxin Ming_ names as the only obstacle to the Way
- **"being master wherever you are"**: 隨處作主 (Linji)

Light imagery is central. Wisdom is a lamp (燈) whose light (光) is inseparable from it. Original mind is a sun and moon hidden by passing clouds. A single lamp ends a thousand years of darkness.

**History.** Much of the tradition's self-history is hagiography.

- **Huineng 慧能 (638–713).** The "Sixth Patriarch" of the _Platform Sutra_ (六祖壇經). The sutra was compiled after his death; the earliest version is the Dunhuang manuscript (c. 780). The standard version is the expanded Zongbao 宗寶 edition (1291). The famous verse contest with Shenxiu is now read as partly Southern-school polemic (McRae, Yampolsky).
- **The _Xinxin Ming_ 信心銘 ("Inscription on Faith in Mind").** Traditionally ascribed to the third patriarch Sengcan 僧璨 (d. c. 606), but probably later (8th c.).
- **Linji Yixuan 臨濟義玄 (d. 866).** Founder of the Linji/Rinzai house; his _Record_ (臨濟錄) was compiled in the Song.
- **Dōgen 道元 (1200–1253).** Brought Caodong/Sōtō to Japan and wrote the _Shōbōgenzō_ 正法眼蔵. _Genjōkōan_ 現成公案 dates to 1233.
- **Ryōkan 良寛 (1758–1831).** A Sōtō monk-hermit and poet, beloved for his simplicity and play with village children.

Zen is a living tradition in East Asia and the West, and its teachers caution against treating its paradoxes as slogans.

## Candidate passages

### 1. Platform Sutra ch. 1 (行由品): Huineng's verse

- **Translation:** Kaiten Nukariya, _The Religion of the Samurai_ (1913), ch. I
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5173/pg5173.txt

> "There is no Bodhi-tree,
> Nor is there a mirror stand.
> Nothing exists from the first
> What can be dimmed by dust and dirt?"

- **Chinese** (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/六祖壇經/行由品): 菩提本無樹，明鏡亦非台，本來無一物，何處惹塵埃。
- **Nukariya's footnote** (same source; worth showing with it): "Mind is pure and bright in its essence. It is always free from passions and mean desires, just as the sun is always bright, despite of cloud and mist that cover its face."
- **Why:** This is the most famous verse in Chan, in a clean PD translation, and the footnote supplies the light imagery. The Goddard 1938 version, for comparison, begins "By no means is Bodhi a kind of tree."

### 2. Platform Sutra ch. 4 (定慧品): samadhi and wisdom are like a lamp and its light

- **Translation:** Wong Mou-lam rev. Goddard, _A Buddhist Bible_ (1938), p. 523
- **Source:** https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.279263/2015.279263.A-Buddhist_djvu.txt (OCR; line-break hyphens joined)

> But we may liken Samadhi and Prajna to a glowing lamp and its light: with the glowing lamp there is light; without it there is darkness. Light is the quintessence of the glowing lamp, the glowing lamp is the expression of light. In name they are two things, but in reality they are one and the same.

- **Chinese** (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/六祖壇經/定慧品): 定慧猶如何等？猶如燈光。有燈即光，無燈即暗；燈是光之體，光是燈之用。名雖有二，體本同一。
- **Why:** This is the Platform Sutra's central image: practice and wisdom are one as a lamp and its light are one. It sets light against darkness explicitly.

### 3. Platform Sutra ch. 4 (定慧品): straightforward mind, not crooked mind with straight lips (sincerity)

- **Translation:** Goddard 1938, p. 523
- **Source:** same as #2

> The Vimalakirti Nirdesa Sutra says: "Straightforwardness is the holy place, the Pure Land." Do not let your mind be "crooked" and try to be straightforward with your lips only.

- **Chinese:** 如淨名經云：「直心是道場，直心是淨土。」莫心行諂曲，口但說直
- **Text note:** the OCR reads "Straight-forwardnesses the holy place". "Straightforwardness is" above is the evident reading; confirm against the page image.
- **Why:** This is Zen's word for sincerity, 直心 _zhíxīn_: integrity between inner mind and outward speech. It is the closest Zen analogue to "he that doeth truth" (John 3:21) and to Shinto _makoto_.

### 4. Platform Sutra ch. 2 (般若品), "formless verse": groping in the dark; not seeing the world's faults (humility)

- **Translation:** Goddard 1938, p. 521–522
- **Source:** same as #2

> If one has not a mind to aim at Buddhahood,
> One will grope in the dark and never find it.
> He who treads the Path in earnest
> Sees not the mistakes of the world.
> If we find fault with others,
> We ourselves are also in the wrong;

- **Chinese** (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/六祖壇經/般若品): 自若無道心　闇行不見道　若真修道人　不見世間過　若見他人非　自非卻是左
- **Why:** This combines both John 3 themes, walking in darkness and the turn from judging others to self-examination. Compare Matthew 7:3.

### 5. Platform Sutra ch. 6 (懺悔品): sun and moon behind passing clouds

- **Translation:** Goddard 1938, p. 527–528
- **Source:** same as #2

> Foolish thoughts may be likened to the clouds while sagacity and Wisdom are the moon and the Sun. When we become attached to discriminated objects, our Mind-essence becomes clouded by drifting thoughts which prevent sagacity and Wisdom from sending forth their light.

- **Chinese** (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/六祖壇經/懺悔品): 如天常清，日月常明，為浮雲蓋覆，上明下暗，忽遇風吹雲散，上下俱明，萬象皆現……智如日，慧如月；智慧常明，於外著境，被妄念浮雲蓋覆，自性不得明朗。
- **Why:** The light is never extinguished, only covered. Compare Dhammapada 173 ("like the moon when freed from clouds").

### 6. Platform Sutra ch. 6 (懺悔品): one lamp dispels a thousand years of darkness

- **Translation:** Goddard 1938, p. 529–530
- **Source:** same as #2

> Let us take the illustration of a lamp. Since the light of a lamp can dissipate darkness that has been there for a thousand years, so a ray of Wisdom can do away with ignorance that has lasted for ages.

- **Chinese:** 譬如一燈能除千年闇，一智能滅萬年愚。
- **Why:** This is short, luminous and hopeful: no darkness is too old for the light. The Chinese is a 12-character couplet, ideal for bilingual display.

### 7. Xinxin Ming 信心銘, opening lines: the Way is not difficult

- **Chinese** (verified): https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/信心銘 (text from 景德傳燈錄 卷30)

> 至道無難　唯嫌揀擇　但莫憎愛　洞然明白　毫釐有差　天地懸隔

- **Researcher's gloss (not a published translation):** "The supreme Way is not difficult; it only dislikes picking and choosing. Only do not hate or love, and it is clear and bright throughout. A hair's breadth of difference, and heaven and earth are set apart."
- **No verified PD English.**
- **Why:** These are among the most quoted lines in Zen. 洞然明白 ("clear and bright throughout") gives the light resonance. A further strong line from the same text: 虛明自照　不勞心力 ("empty and bright, it illumines itself, without effort of mind"; researcher's gloss).

### 8. Record of Linji: no pleasure at Buddhas, no fear of hells (death and fearlessness)

- **Translation:** Nukariya, _The Religion of the Samurai_ (1913), ch. on "Life"/"Enlightenment"; he marks the ellipsis himself
- **Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5173/pg5173.txt

> "All the Buddhas might appear before me and I would not be glad. All the Three Regions and Hells might suddenly present themselves before me, and I would not fear. . . . He (an Enlightened person) might get into the fire, and it would not burn him. He might get into water, and it would not drown him.

- **Chinese** (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/鎮州臨濟慧照禪師語錄): 十方諸佛現前無一念心喜，三塗地獄頓現無一念心怖。……入火不燒、入水不溺
- **Why:** This is a PD English Linji saying on freedom from fear, including fear of death and hell. Nukariya's paraphrase is loose (it compresses the passage), so if it is displayed, show the Chinese alongside.

### 9. Record of Linji: be master wherever you are

- **Chinese** (verified): https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/鎮州臨濟慧照禪師語錄

> 爾且隨處作主，立處皆真，境來回換不得

- **Researcher's gloss:** "Just be master wherever you are, and wherever you stand is true; circumstances that come cannot turn you around."
- **No verified PD English.** The Sasaki and Watson translations are copyrighted.
- **Why:** 隨處作主，立處皆真 is one of the most cited Chan phrases, and _wherever you stand is true_ resonates with "doing the truth."

### 10. Dōgen, _Genjōkōan_ 現成公案 (1233): to study the self is to forget the self

- **Japanese:** from the Sōtō Zen Text Project edition, https://www.sotozen.com/eng/library/shobogenzo/pdf/vol1_1.pdf

> 佛道をならふといふは、自己をならふなり。自己をならふといふは、自己をわするるなり。自己をわするるといふは、萬法に證せらるるなり。

- **English:** Sōtō Zen Text Project (2023), **copyrighted, all rights reserved; do not display without permission**. Same PDF: "To study the way of the buddhas is to study oneself. To study oneself is to forget oneself. To forget oneself is to be verified by the myriad dharmas."
- **Why:** This is the single most quoted Dōgen passage, on humility as self-forgetting. Display requires a licensed or owner-made translation.

### 11. Dōgen, _Genjōkōan_: awakening is like the moon in water

- **Japanese:** same PDF as #10

> 人の、さとりをうる、水に月のやどるがごとし。月ぬれず、水やぶれず。ひろくおほきなるひかりにてあれど、尺寸の水にやどり、全月も彌天も、くさの露にもやどり、一滴の水にもやどる。

- **English:** Sōtō Zen Text Project (2023), **copyrighted**: "A person's attaining of awakening is like the moon residing in the water. The moon does not get wet, and the water is not disturbed. Although its illumination is wide and great, it resides in water of a foot or an inch. Even the whole moon and all the heavens reside in the dew on a blade of grass or reside in a single drop of water."
- **Why:** Light dwelling in the humblest vessel makes a beautiful counterpart to "the light is come into the world" (John 3:19).

### 12. Ryōkan, death verse

- **Japanese** (verified via https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/良寛, "辞世" section, citing 定本良寛全集 vol. 3; recorded by the nun Teishin 貞心尼 in 『蓮の露』):

> うらを見せおもてを見せてちるもみぢ

- **Researcher's gloss:** "Showing its back, then showing its face — a maple leaf falls."
- **Note:** the verse is carved on a stone at Entsū-ji. Some scholars think Ryōkan was quoting rather than composing it. The same Wikipedia section notes he did not compose it on his deathbed. His farewell waka is also recorded there: 形見とて何残すらむ春は花夏ほととぎす秋はもみぢ葉 ("As a keepsake, what shall I leave? Flowers in spring, the cuckoo in summer, maple leaves in autumn"; researcher's gloss).
- **Why:** This is a gentle Zen view of death and of showing one's whole self, both sides, without concealment, which echoes John 3:21's "made manifest."

## Bridges

1. **Lamp and light vs. darkness.** Platform Sutra #2 and #6 pair with **Christianity, John 3:19–21** and **John 1:5** ("the light shineth in darkness"). The Buddhist **Dhammapada 146** asks "Why do you not seek a light?" The tension: in Zen the light is one's _own_ original nature, not light that "is come into the world" from outside.
2. **Sincerity: straight mind.** Platform Sutra #3 (直心; mind not crooked while lips speak straight) pairs with the **Confucian Doctrine of the Mean** ch. 20 and 25 on 誠 _chéng_ ("sincerity is the way of Heaven") and with **Shinto** _makoto_ 誠 (see the bushido file). It also echoes **Matthew 15:8** ("This people … honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me").
3. **Not seeing others' faults.** Platform Sutra #4 pairs with **Matthew 7:3–5** (mote and beam) and **Dhammapada 252**.
4. **Clouds over the sun.** Platform Sutra #5 pairs with **Dhammapada 173** (the moon freed from clouds) and **Mencius 6A:8** (Ox Mountain: original goodness obscured, not destroyed). Both share the view that human nature is fundamentally bright or good, a tension with **Xunzi**'s "human nature is bad" (性惡) and with Augustinian original sin.
5. **Forgetting the self.** Dōgen #10 pairs with **Mark 8:35** (whoever loses his life will save it), **Zhuangzi** ch. 6's "sitting and forgetting" (坐忘) and **Meister Eckhart**'s detachment.
6. **Death.** Ryōkan #12 and Linji #8 pair with **Hagakure** (constant readiness for death, but Zen-inflected), the **Buddha's last words in DN 16** and **Zhuangzi** ch. 18 (drumming on a basin after his wife's death).

## Beware (misattributions and slogan traps)

- **"Before enlightenment, chop wood, carry water; after enlightenment, chop wood, carry water."** A modern popular formulation with no fixed classical source. The genuine near-source is Layman Pang's verse 神通并妙用，運水及搬柴 ("supernatural power and marvelous function: drawing water and carrying firewood"; not fetched here).
- **"When the student is ready, the teacher will appear."** Theosophical/New Age in origin, not Zen or Buddhist.
- **"Wherever you go, there you are."** Modern (popularized by Jon Kabat-Zinn's 1994 book title), not a Zen classic.
- **"If you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him."** Genuine Linji in substance (逢佛殺佛、逢祖殺祖…, verified in the zh.wikisource Record), but it is a teaching against clinging to external authorities. Out of context it reads as violent and is a poor fit for this site. "On the road" is a modern addition (Sheldon Kopp's 1972 book title).
- **"The obstacle is the path"** and **"Let go or be dragged"** are labelled "Zen proverbs" with no classical source.
- **"Not knowing is most intimate"** (不知最親切) is genuine (Dizang to Fayan, _Book of Equanimity_ case 20), but not verified here.
- **Huineng's verse:** popular versions conflate the Dunhuang and Zongbao readings. The Dunhuang text has 佛性常清淨 ("Buddha-nature is always pure") in place of 本來無一物 ("from the first not a thing exists"). Cite the edition.

## Unverified (memory only)

- **Ryōkan, 盗人に取り残されし窓の月** ("The thief left it behind — the moon at the window"; researcher's gloss). This is a widely loved haiku attributed to Ryōkan, a strong light candidate. I could not locate it in a fetched primary or reference source in this session. Verify in 定本良寛全集 before use.
- **Wong Mou-lam's original 1930 wording.** Commonly quoted as "There is no Bodhi-tree, / Nor stand of a mirror bright…", but that may be the 1944/1953 revision. The 1930 text was not fetched, so do not use until a 1930 scan is checked.
